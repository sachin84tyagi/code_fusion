## Chapter Overview

Chapter 13 was the complete accumulation roadmap — how professional money enters at the bottom. Chapter 14 is the exact mirror: how professional money exits at the top. The distribution schematic is structurally identical to the accumulation schematic but inverted in direction and reversed in psychology.

Where accumulation exploits public FEAR, distribution exploits public GREED. Where accumulation absorbs supply, distribution absorbs demand. Where the Spring tests support, the Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD) tests resistance. The mechanism is the same; the direction is opposite.

For NSE traders, distribution analysis is equally important because:
1. Identifying distribution allows exit from long positions BEFORE the SOW breakdown
2. For F&O traders, distribution provides the highest-confidence short setups
3. Understanding distribution prevents "buying the dip" into a declining stock

**The Chapter 14 Rule:**

> **Distribution looks bullish from the outside — new ATHs, positive news, retail euphoria. The Wyckoff analyst must read BENEATH the surface: volume behavior, delivery %, and close location on climactic bars tell the true story that price alone cannot.**

## LEVEL 1 — BEGINNER

### 14.1 The Distribution Reference Framework

![Wyckoff Distribution Schematic — Complete Phase Map (Phases A through E)](/images/pi-distribution-schematic-full.jpg)

**The two key reference levels in distribution:**

```
Top Reference — "The Ice" (BC High):
The high of the Buying Climax.
This becomes the TOP of the distribution range.
Price may briefly exceed this level via the UTAD — but closes back below.
This is the top of the distribution zone — the level the CO sold into.

Bottom Reference — "The Creek" (AR Low):
The low of the Automatic Reaction after the BC.
This becomes the BOTTOM of the distribution range.
When price BREAKS BELOW the Creek on high volume (SOW), markdown begins.
```

**The mirror comparison:**

| Accumulation | Distribution |
|-------------|-------------|
| Ice = SC Low (range bottom) | Ice = BC High (range top) |
| Creek = AR High (range top, resistance) | Creek = AR Low (range bottom, support) |
| Spring dips below the Ice | UTAD spikes above the Ice |
| SOS jumps above the Creek | SOW breaks below the Creek |
| LPS holds the Creek as new support | LPSY fails at the Creek as new resistance |

### 14.2 Phase A — Stopping the Uptrend

**Purpose:** Halt the prior uptrend. The first meaningful supply appears. The range boundaries are defined.

**A.1 — Preliminary Supply (PSY)**

*The first noticeable slowdown in the uptrend.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Moderate to wide (up bar, but price gives back much of the gain)
Volume:  Elevated (first meaningful supply interest)
Close:   Middle or lower half of the bar's range (upper wick)
Background: Uptrend of 8+ weeks, volume rising on prior up-bars

Mechanics:
→ First significant selling meets the rising demand
→ An upper wick forms — buyers pushed price up but sellers capped it
→ Volume elevated but NOT climactic
→ Price may still close higher than open (technically "green")
   BUT the CLOSE LOCATION is what matters — middle or below = supply appeared

Significance: OBSERVATION only — early warning sign.
              Not all distributions show a clear PSY before the BC.
              The absence of PSY does not invalidate a subsequent BC.
```

**A.2 — Buying Climax (BC)**

*The top event. Maximum greed. Climactic buying absorbed by professional supply.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Widest bar in the prior 4–8 weeks (often an ATH intrabar)
Volume:  Climactic — highest in 4–8 weeks (typically > 5× recent average)
Close:   Middle to lower half of the bar (significant upper wick — price fell from high)
Background: Extended uptrend, high public enthusiasm, news is positive

Mechanics:
→ Retail FOMO buyers all rush in simultaneously — the final wave of demand
→ Market buy orders cascade through a thin ask side → wide range
→ Professional seller distributes their ENTIRE accumulated position into this buying
→ Upper wick = intrabar price extended to ATH but FELL BACK as selling absorbed demand
→ Close in middle = buyers were powerful (wide range) BUT sellers were
   powerful enough to push price BELOW the intrabar high significantly

The BC at the ATH: Why a new ATH can be bearish
→ The public EXPECTS a new ATH to be bullish (continuation of trend)
→ But if the ATH is on extreme volume and the close falls back → it's the CO
   completing distribution into the last wave of retail buying

NSE-specific BC checks:
→ Delivery %: Should be LOW (< 30–35%) — retail FOMO buyers often exit next day
→ Institutional net flow: FII may show net SELLING on the BC day
→ Options data: Put-Call ratio falls sharply as calls are aggressively bought
   (retail is bullish at the BC; institution is selling)
→ Bulk/Block deals: Large block trades may appear (promoter offloading, QIP at the top)
→ Earnings/Event: Is there a catalyst? If so, apply extra caution to the BC interpretation
```

**A.3 — Automatic Reaction (AR)**

*The first significant decline from the BC.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Moderate to wide (down bars)
Volume:  LOWER than the BC (confirms BC was genuine supply)
Close:   Lower half of each bar in the AR sequence
Background: Immediately follows the BC bar

Mechanics:
→ BC exhausted demand — buyers who intended to buy just bought everything
→ Early longs take profits after the ATH (selling)
→ With demand gone, even modest selling drives price down rapidly
→ AR defines the BOTTOM of the distribution range (the Creek)

CRITICAL RULE: AR volume must be LOWER than BC volume.
If AR volume > BC volume:
→ More selling than the BC had buying = unusual
→ More likely: BC was not the genuine distribution top
→ Another, more extreme BC may follow (or the distribution continues higher)

Expected AR duration: 3–10 sessions
Expected decline: 5–15% from BC high
```

**A.4 — Secondary Test (ST)**

*The market revisits the BC area to confirm demand is absent.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow (much narrower than BC)
Volume:  LOW — often lowest in 15–20+ sessions
Close:   Lower half of the ST bar (fails to hold near the BC high)
Background: Follows AR; price rallies from AR low toward BC area

Mechanics:
→ Price rallies from the AR low (natural bounce after the drop)
→ As it approaches the BC area, volume dries up completely
→ Ultra-low volume = almost no buyers willing to buy at this level
→ Sellers cap the rally — close in lower half
→ Next session: price moves AWAY from the BC area lower

Criteria for a PASSING ST (bearish — confirms BC):
✓ Volume is the LOWEST of the past 15–20 sessions on the rally
✓ Price holds BELOW the BC high (or barely exceeds it by < 1%)
✓ Close in lower half of the ST bar
✓ Following session moves away lower (does not immediately push to new high)

Criteria for a FAILING ST (BC may not be genuine top):
✗ Volume is high on the rally toward BC area (buyers ARE present)
✗ Price closes ABOVE the BC high significantly
✗ Following session continues higher → possible trend continuation
→ The BC may have been a PSY (Preliminary Supply), not the final distribution top
```

### 14.3 Phase B — Building the Cause (Distribution Edition)

**Purpose:** The Composite Operator distributes their entire long position. Demand is methodically absorbed as supply is placed.

**Duration:** The LONGEST phase — weeks to months proportional to the size of the position being distributed.

**What happens in Phase B:**

```
Price action: Oscillates within the range (between AR Low and BC High)
              Range structure deteriorates internally — LH/LL within range
              (Opposite of accumulation's internal HH/HL improvement)

Volume:  DECLINING overall
         Occasional absorption spikes when CO distributes into buying rallies
         Down-day volume trending higher than up-day volume (opposite of accumulation)
         Delivery %: Steadily FALLING as committed holders reduce positions

VSA events inside Phase B:
→ Multiple Secondary Tests (ST) — each on progressively lower volume
→ No Demand bars (narrow, low volume, lower close) on rallies
→ Minor Upthrusts within the range (false breaks above BC High)
   — these are distributed into, then fall back
→ Absorption at top: High volume bars at BC level with UPPER WICKS
   (CO selling into buying interest near the top)

The story of Phase B (distribution):
"The Composite Operator has 10 million shares accumulated at ₹400.
Current price: ₹1,200 (300% up from accumulation). They need to exit.
If they sell all 10 million at once, price collapses against them.
So: They sell gradually every time buyers appear near the BC high.
    The public keeps buying the dips (believing the uptrend will resume).
    The CO obliges — selling INTO each bounce, week after week.
    This goes on until demand is exhausted."
```

**Phase B distribution diagnostic checklist:**

- [ ] Price oscillating within BC High to AR Low range
- [ ] Overall volume declining from Phase A levels
- [ ] Each rally toward BC area occurs on LOWER volume than prior rally
- [ ] Each rally toward BC area FAILS at the same or LOWER level
- [ ] Delivery % shows a gradual declining trend
- [ ] Price is making LH and LL within the range (internal structure deteriorating)
- [ ] Down-day average volume > Up-day average volume (supply dominant)

**Duration guide:**

```
Phase B duration (weeks): Distribution size estimate
4–6 weeks:   Small position being distributed (smaller subsequent decline)
8–12 weeks:  Moderate position (meaningful decline ahead)
14–20 weeks: Large position (significant decline — major top)
> 20 weeks:  Very large position (multi-month markdown likely)
```

### 14.4 Phase C — Testing Demand

**Purpose:** One final test to confirm that ALL demand has been absorbed. The UTAD.

**C.1 — Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD)**

*The mirror of the Spring.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Wide (bar reaches new ATH briefly — above the BC High)
Volume:  Moderate to high (varies — see types below)
Close:   BACK BELOW the BC High (the key criterion)
Background: Phase B complete; price approaches the BC High again

Mechanics:
→ Price briefly pushes ABOVE the BC High (the Ice / distribution range top)
→ This triggers: Buy-stop orders from breakout traders
→ This causes: Short-covering from early bears
→ But: Demand is GONE (absorbed in Phases A and B)
→ Result: Price immediately FALLS BACK below the BC High
→ The UTAD proves: No genuine buyers remain above the BC level

"All the remaining demand — the breakout buyers, the short coverers —
appears briefly above the BC High. The CO absorbs every last bit of it,
distributing their final units. Then price collapses. Demand is done."

TYPE 1 UTAD (highest confidence):
→ Volume HIGH on the push above BC High (genuine demand appeared and was absorbed)
→ Close falls sharply back below the BC High
→ Strong follow-through lower in next session(s)

TYPE 2 UTAD (moderate confidence):
→ Volume MODERATE on the push above BC High
→ Still closes back below BC High
→ May require additional No Demand confirmation

UTAD criteria checklist:
✓ Price pushes ABOVE the BC High (even briefly — intrabar breach is sufficient)
✓ Close falls BACK BELOW the BC High (decisive close back inside or below range)
✓ Volume confirms the absorption (high for Type 1)
✓ Following session continues lower or consolidates near the UTAD close
✓ Prior Phase B shows deteriorating structure (LH/LL within range)
```

**C.2 — No Demand (ND) Test**

*The mirror of the Positive Test in accumulation.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow
Volume:  ULTRA-LOW (lowest in 15–20 sessions)
Close:   Lower half (below 50% of bar's range)
Background: After UTAD; price is near the BC High area

Mechanics:
→ A probe of the BC area confirms: no buyers present
→ Ultra-low volume = almost no demand remaining
→ Close in lower half = sellers hold the marginal advantage
→ This is the confirmation that the UTAD absorbed the final demand

No Demand criteria:
✓ Volume ultra-low (< 0.4× average, ideally the lowest in 20 sessions)
✓ Close in lower half (< 50% of bar's range)
✓ Narrow spread
✓ Price holds BELOW the BC High

Phase C complete when: UTAD occurred + No Demand confirmation (or the UTAD itself was on very high volume, confirming absorption in a single session).
```

## LEVEL 2 — INTERMEDIATE

### 14.5 Phase D — Supply Takes Control

**D.1 — Sign of Weakness (SOW) / "Break Through the Ice"**

*The mirror of the SOS.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Wide (down bar — > 1.5× ATR)
Volume:  High (2–4× recent average)
Close:   Bottom third (< 25% of bar's range)
Background: Phase C complete (UTAD + No Demand confirmed)

Mechanics:
→ Supply now has NO opposition — demand exhausted in Phases A/B/C
→ Any selling immediately drives price lower (no buyers absorbing)
→ Price BREAKS BELOW the Creek (AR Low) on wide range, high volume
→ This is the structural announcement: distribution is complete, markdown begins

"The Ice (BC High) was the top that capped all rallies.
The Creek (AR Low) was the floor that had held during distribution.
The SOW breaks THROUGH the Creek — the floor gives way.
Markdown has begun."

SOW criteria checklist:
✓ Volume 2–4× recent average
✓ Range wide (> 1.5× ATR)
✓ Close in bottom 25% of range
✓ Bar closes BELOW the AR Low (Creek)
✓ Prior Phase C confirmed (UTAD + No Demand)
✓ Delivery % elevated (committed sellers, not just intraday shorts)
```

**D.2 — Last Point of Supply (LPSY)**

*The mirror of the LPS — the optimal SHORT entry.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow (small range on the bounce)
Volume:  LOW (< 0.7× average)
Close:   Lower half (holds below old Creek support)
Background: After SOW; price bounces weakly toward the Creek level

Mechanics:
→ After breaking the Creek (SOW), a weak bounce naturally occurs
→ The bounce reaches toward the AR Low level (old support)
→ The old support is now tested as RESISTANCE (role reversal)
→ Low volume on the bounce = no genuine demand returning
→ Close in lower half = sellers still in control
→ The LPSY is the proof that the SOW was genuine — supply remains dominant

LPSY criteria checklist:
✓ Follows SOW within 2–8 sessions
✓ Volume LOW (< 0.7× average, ideally lowest since the UTAD)
✓ Price holds BELOW the Creek (AR Low level)
✓ Close in lower half of the LPSY bar
✓ Following session continues lower

THE LPSY IS THE OPTIMAL SHORT ENTRY IN THE ENTIRE DISTRIBUTION CYCLE.
(Mirror of the LPS being the optimal LONG entry in accumulation)
```

**D.3 — BUEC (Back Up to Edge of Creek — Distribution)**

*Subsequent bounces to the Creek level (now resistance) during early Phase E.*

```
Each additional bounce to the AR Low level that FAILS = BUEC (bearish)
→ Low volume on the bounce
→ Close in lower half
→ Confirms the Creek has truly become resistance
→ Each failed BUEC is an additional short entry opportunity
```

### 14.6 Phase E — Markdown and Short Trade Management

**Markdown characteristics:**

```
Price structure: LH/LL sequence (Chapter 7 downtrend)
Volume pattern: Expanding on down-legs, contracting on bounces (LPSYs)
Duration: Weeks to months

Within Phase E:
→ Re-distribution ranges (pauses in the decline — smaller ranges)
→ Each re-distribution has the same structure as primary distribution
   but shorter and at a lower price level
→ Each re-distribution adds ADDITIONAL cause to the original
→ Combined cause increases the downside target
```

**Phase E — accumulation warning signals (covering shorts):**

```
Signal 1: Volume divergence on down-legs
→ Each successive down-wave declines on LESS volume (demand absorbing supply)
→ This is the mirror of the accumulation signal

Signal 2: Down-bars narrowing despite high volume
→ Supply is being absorbed (high volume, narrow range)
→ Potential SC forming

Signal 3: Price makes a new low that HOLDS (no follow-through lower)
→ Potential Preliminary Support (PS)

Signal 4: Wide down-bar with LOWER WICK recovery
→ Potential SC event (beginning of accumulation Phase A)

Signal 5: Structural CHoCH upward
→ Price breaks ABOVE a prior Lower High (LH) for the first time
→ STRONGEST structural signal that markdown is ending
→ Exit short positions immediately or trail aggressively

Management rule: Cover shorts at T1 (1× range below SOW), take partial profits.
                 Cover remaining at T2 (2× range) or on SC + AR signal.
                 Never hold through a CHoCH upward — that is the invalidation.
```

### 14.7 The Three Short Entry Zones

![Distribution Short Trade Planning — Entry Zones, Stops, and Targets](/images/pi-distribution-short-zones.jpg)

**Short Entry Zone 1 (S1) — UTAD / Upthrust Entry**

```
Timing: During Phase C — after the UTAD bar closes back below the BC High

Trigger:
→ UTAD bar's close: Back below the BC High resistance level
→ Volume on the UTAD: High (confirms absorption of breakout buying)
→ Next session: Does NOT immediately re-attempt the BC High level

Entry price: On the close of the UTAD bar (if high-volume close back below)
             Or: At the open of the FOLLOWING session (after confirmation)
             Aggressive traders: Short the UTAD bar intraday at the BC High level
             (with stop above the UTAD High intrabar)

Stop-loss (SL): Above the UTAD HIGH
                This is typically 1–3% above the BC High
                (Tight stop — if price exceeds UTAD high with another close above,
                the distribution thesis may be wrong)

Target 1 (T1): AR Low (Creek) — the range bottom
Target 2 (T2): Cause-Effect downside projection below the Creek

Instrument: Stock futures (short futures) or Put options (ATM or 1 strike OTM)
            AVOID: Cash market shorting (NSE cash market requires delivery for shorting;
                   intraday sell only — position cannot be carried overnight easily)

Position size: 40–50% of intended full short position
              (UTAD is early — UTAD confirmation needed; distribution thesis not fully confirmed)

Risk-Reward: 1:3 to 1:8 (tight stop above UTAD High; large potential decline ahead)
```

**Short Entry Zone 2 (S2) — LPSY Entry (Optimal)**

```
Timing: During Phase D — after SOW, on the weak bounce to the Creek

Trigger:
→ SOW has occurred (all criteria met)
→ Bounce toward the Creek begins (LPSY forming)
→ Volume dries up on the bounce (RelVol < 0.7)
→ Bounce bar closes in the LOWER half of its range
→ Following session begins to move AWAY from the Creek downward

Entry price: At the close of the LPSY bar, or at the open of next session

Stop-loss (SL): Above the LPSY High (tightest)
                OR: Above the Creek level — AR Low (moderate stop)
                OR: Above the SOW bar's midpoint (widest stop)

Target 1 (T1): 1× Range height BELOW the SOW breakdown level
Target 2 (T2): 2× Range height (for Phase B durations > 8 weeks)

Position size: 75–100% of intended full short position
              (LPSY is the highest-confidence short — SOW confirmed, role-reversal confirmed)

Risk-Reward: 1:4 to 1:8

NSE instruments for S2:
→ Stock futures (SHORT futures — ideal for overnight carry)
→ Put options: Buy ATM puts with expiry > 3 weeks away
→ Avoid call spreads unless volatility is very high
```

**Short Entry Zone 3 (S3) — Re-Distribution Entry**

```
Timing: During Phase E — on any re-distribution LPSY within the markdown

Trigger:
→ Markdown is underway (price below the Creek, LH/LL confirmed)
→ A re-distribution range forms (price consolidates briefly)
→ Re-distribution shows same Phase A/B/C/D structure (BC/AR/UTAD/SOW)
→ LPSY of the re-distribution = new short entry

Entry: On the re-distribution LPSY close
Stop: Above the re-distribution range top
Target: Next downside Cause-Effect level (cumulative cause)

Position size: 25–50% ADD to existing S2 position
              (Markdown confirmed — add to winners)
```

**Staged position building for short trades:**

```
S1 (UTAD): 40–50% position — early, tight stop above UTAD High
S2 (LPSY): Add to 75–100% — optimal confirmed setup
S3 (Re-distribution): Optional 25–50% add — markdown fully confirmed

Average entry price calculation example:
UTAD short at ₹1,050 (40% position)
LPSY short at ₹990 (add to 100%)
Average short price: ₹1,014

UTAD Hard Stop: ₹1,068 (UTAD High + 1%)
LPSY Stop: ₹1,005 (AR Low = ₹1,000 + buffer)

Target 1 (T1): ₹900 (1× range: BC ₹1,100 − AR ₹1,000 = ₹100 below SOW at ₹1,000 → ₹900)
Target 2 (T2): ₹800 (2× range, Phase B duration > 10 weeks)
```

### 14.8 NSE-Specific Short-Selling Mechanics

**The NSE short-selling landscape:**

```
INSTRUMENT 1: CASH MARKET (Intraday only)
→ Can sell stocks in the cash market intraday (SELL first, BUY back same day)
→ Cannot carry a short overnight in the cash market without delivery
→ BTST (Buy Today Sell Tomorrow): Works for longs only
→ STBT (Sell Today Buy Tomorrow): NOT available for retail in Indian cash market
→ USE: Intraday UTAD short on the day it appears
→ AVOID: Overnight/positional shorts in cash market

INSTRUMENT 2: STOCK FUTURES (NSE F&O)
→ SHORT futures: Sell a futures contract, buy back later
→ Can be held overnight and across weeks (roll contracts at expiry)
→ Margin required: Approximately 15–25% of contract value (SPAN + Exposure)
→ Settlement: Cash-settled (no delivery issues)
→ IDEAL for: UTAD entry (S1), LPSY entry (S2), Re-distribution (S3)
→ Roll cost: Minimal for monthly contracts; watch spread near expiry

INSTRUMENT 3: PUT OPTIONS
→ Buy put options instead of short futures
→ Maximum loss: Premium paid (defined risk — vs futures which have unlimited risk)
→ Strike selection: ATM or 1 strike ITM puts for best delta capture
→ Expiry: Minimum 3 weeks remaining (avoid very short-dated puts — theta decay)
→ Entry: S1 UTAD day → Buy puts (cost of protection is high on big moves)
   S2 LPSY → Buy puts (lower IV, better entry)
→ Greeks consideration: Delta 0.4–0.6, Vega risk if IV falls after entry

INSTRUMENT 4: NIFTY/SENSEX FUTURES + SECTORAL INDEX FUTURES
→ When Nifty itself is in distribution: Short Nifty futures
→ Less stock-specific risk; correlated with market-wide distribution
→ Sector futures (Nifty Bank, Nifty IT): Sector-specific distribution plays
```

**NSE Short-sell practical checklist:**

```
Before executing any short:
□ Confirmed distribution schematic (SC, AR, ST minimum + Phase B deterioration)
□ Nine Selling Tests score: 6+ out of 9
□ Instrument chosen: Futures / Puts (avoid cash market for positional shorts)
□ Expiry date verified (at least 3 weeks for puts, no same-day expiry)
□ Stop-loss calculated (in ₹ AND as % of capital)
□ Position size verified (max 5% portfolio loss if stopped out)
□ Target calculated (T1 and T2 in specific prices)
□ FII/DII data checked (FII net sellers for the past 5 sessions?)
□ Not an earnings/event week (if yes, reduce size or wait until after)
□ Not F&O expiry week for the UTAD (volume distorted)
```

## LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED / PROFESSIONAL

### 14.9 Non-Classic Distribution Variations

**Variation 1: Distribution Without a UTAD**

```
When: Demand was so thoroughly exhausted in Phase B that no final
      push above the BC High was needed.

Evidence:
→ Multiple STs in Phase B, each with progressively lower volume
→ Final STs show near-zero volume on any rally toward BC area
→ No Demand bars dominating Phase B's later portion
→ SOW appears directly from within the range (no UTAD preceding)

The SOW in this case: Should be on particularly high volume (3.5×+)
                       AND the No Demand confirmation before the SOW
                       should be particularly convincing (ultra-low volume)

Trade implication:
→ S1 entry (UTAD) is unavailable — move directly to S2 (LPSY)
→ Require SOW volume to be 3.5×+ to compensate for missing UTAD
→ Nine Selling Tests: Test 5 (upside breaks failing) may not pass clearly
→ Reduce confidence by 1 test score level; require higher SOW/LPSY confirmation

Confidence: Moderate-High if STs showed ultra-low volume throughout Phase B
```

**Variation 2: Multiple UTADs (Phase C Extends)**

```
When: Demand repeatedly reappears above the BC High.

Evidence:
→ UTAD 1 occurs, but volume is LOW (demand barely appeared above BC)
→ Price falls from UTAD 1 but FAILS to produce an SOW
→ Price rallies again, exceeds BC High again — UTAD 2
→ UTAD 2 should show LOWER volume than UTAD 1 (demand diminishing)
→ If UTAD 2 volume is HIGHER → distribution continues; demand still present

Trade implication:
→ Wait for the LAST UTAD to show the lowest volume
→ Progressive: UTAD 1 (high vol) → UTAD 2 (lower vol) → No Demand (ultra-low)
→ Then SOW confirms Phase D

Pattern of multiple UTADs = the CO is having difficulty absorbing demand above the range
= The distribution has strong demand opposing it = more Phase B time needed
```

**Variation 3: Slow SOW / Weak-Volume Breakdown**

```
When: The SOW happens without a dramatic high-volume climactic bar.
      Price just drifts below the Creek with moderate volume.

Evidence:
→ No single wide, high-volume break below the AR Low
→ Instead: Successive moderate down-bars that gradually erode below the Creek
→ Cumulative volume on the break = high (3–4 sessions of moderate volume)

This indicates: Supply is steady but not concentrated; distributed shorts
                from multiple participants, not one large seller

Trade implication:
→ The LPSY is LESS obvious (price drifts lower, no dramatic bounce)
→ Watch for the first attempted rally after the Creek break on low volume
→ This first low-volume rally after the break = LPSY equivalent
→ Higher chance of a slower, drawn-out markdown (not sharp vertical decline)
```

**Variation 4: Failed Distribution (Phase D Continues Up)**

```
When: The distribution FAILS — price breaks ABOVE the BC High on high volume
      and does NOT return below.

Signs of failure:
→ A UTAD candidate occurs (price briefly above BC High)
→ But: Instead of closing back below, price CONTINUES HIGHER for 2+ sessions
→ Volume on the break above is HIGH (2–3×) AND SUSTAINED (not a spike-and-fall)
→ A new Automatic Rally forms (new AR High) — distribution is re-drawing its range at a higher level

Interpretation:
→ The BC was actually a Preliminary Supply (PSY) event
→ A new, higher BC will form at the extended price level
→ The distribution range re-anchors at the new high
→ Exit any shorts immediately — the "distribution" was incorrect

NSE practical rule:
If any UTAD candidate is followed by price closing ABOVE the BC High
for TWO CONSECUTIVE sessions on volume > 1.5× average:
→ The distribution thesis is INVALIDATED
→ Cover shorts without exception
```

### 14.10 Distribution Schematic — The Real-Time Decision Tree

```
STEP 1: Is there a prior uptrend of ≥ 8 weeks?
→ YES: Potential Phase A beginning
→ NO: Possible re-distribution (smaller cause) or not a distribution top

STEP 2: Has a climactic event occurred (wide spread, extreme volume, upper wick)?
→ YES: BC identified → Phase A → Enter Phase B watch
→ NO: Still in uptrend OR PSY only → Wait for BC

STEP 3: Has the AR defined a clear range bottom? Has an ST occurred on low volume?
→ BOTH YES: Phase A complete, Phase B beginning
→ ST on HIGH volume (rally to BC area): BC may not be genuine → Wait

STEP 4: Is price oscillating between BC High and AR Low with declining volume?
→ YES: Phase B ongoing → Count duration, watch delivery %
→ Break ABOVE range on HIGH volume and SUSTAINED: Failed distribution → Cover shorts

STEP 5: Has a UTAD occurred? (Push above BC High + close back below)
→ YES, high volume: Phase C → Wait for No Demand confirmation
→ YES, low volume: Phase C (Type 2 UTAD) → No Demand REQUIRED
→ NO UTAD, price just falling: Possible no-UTAD distribution

STEP 6: Has No Demand confirmed? (Ultra-low volume near BC area, lower close)
→ YES: Phase C complete → Watch for SOW
→ NO: Phase C incomplete → Do NOT enter shorts yet (for most traders)

STEP 7: Has the SOW broken below the Creek on high volume?
→ YES: Phase D begins → Wait for LPSY
→ Low volume on the break: Questionable SOW → Wait for second confirmation

STEP 8: Has the LPSY formed? (Low volume bounce to Creek fails, lower close)
→ YES: OPTIMAL SHORT ENTRY (S2) → Execute trade plan
→ Bounce on HIGH volume: LPSY failed → Possible re-accumulation forming

STEP 9: Is markdown underway? (LH/LL below the Creek)
→ YES: Phase E → Manage short with trailing stops
→ Re-distribution forming: S3 add-on opportunity
```

### 14.11 Distribution vs Re-Accumulation at the Same Range — The Critical Distinction

This is the most commercially costly identification error in Wyckoff analysis. A range at a HIGH price level can be either:
- **Distribution** — if the prior uptrend is ending (CO selling) → Markdown follows
- **Re-Accumulation** — if the prior uptrend is pausing (CO holding and adding) → Markup continues

The stakes are high: a misidentified distribution = shorting into a continuation up move. A misidentified re-accumulation = holding longs into a markdown.

**The seven-criterion decision matrix:**

| Criterion | Points to Distribution | Points to Re-Accumulation |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Volume at range TOP | High (absorption of buying) | Low (no demand at resistance) |
| Volume at range BOTTOM | Low (no supply returning) | High (Spring — supply briefly tested and rejected) |
| Internal structure | LH/LL within range | HH/HL within range |
| Break BELOW range | High volume (genuine SOW) | Low volume (Spring — reverses immediately) |
| Break ABOVE range | Low volume or fails (UT) | High volume (SOS — confirmed) |
| Delivery % trend | Declining | Rising |
| Relative strength vs Nifty | Weakening | Stable or strengthening |

**Scoring:** Each criterion pointing clearly one way = 1 point. 5+/7 = high confidence in that direction. 3-4/7 = ambiguous — wait.

### 14.12 Complete Distribution Trade Management

**Short stop management:**

```
Stage 1 (UTAD short entry): Hard stop = UTAD High
Stage 2 (LPSY entry): Move stop to just above the LPSY High (tighter)
Stage 3 (First re-distribution LPSY): Move stop to just above that LPSY High
Stage 4 (Each subsequent re-distribution): Move stop to each new Lower High

Trailing rule for markdown:
→ Each confirmed Lower High (LH) in the markdown = new stop level
→ Stop is placed 1–2% ABOVE the most recent confirmed LH
→ When price makes a new LH and holds for 2+ sessions: Move stop down

The stop DESCENDS with each new LH — mirror of LPS trailing stop ascending in markup.
```

**Profit-taking for short trades:**

```
T1 (First target): Cover 25–33% of short at 1× range below SOW breakdown
                   Lock in profit — reduces risk as price approaches potential SC area

T2 (Second target): Cover another 25–33% at 2× range (> 10-week Phase B)

T3 (Markdown ending signals): Exit ALL shorts when:
→ Volume divergence on down-legs (decreasing volume on new lows)
→ Wide down-bar with LOWER WICK recovery (potential SC)
→ Delivery % spiking on a down-day (committed buying at lows)
→ CHoCH upward (breaks above prior Lower High)
→ FII net buying appearing after sustained net selling
→ Nine Buying Tests scoring 5+/9 (accumulation forming)
```

## QUIZ ANSWERS

### Conceptual Answers

**A1.** Two reference levels: (1) The Ice = BC High = Top of distribution range. Used as: the ceiling that the CO distributed into; the level that the UTAD briefly exceeds before closing back below; the resistance for all subsequent rally attempts in Phase D/E. (2) The Creek = AR Low = Bottom of distribution range. Used as: the floor during distribution oscillation; the level that the SOW breaks below; the resistance for LPSY bounces (role reversal from support to resistance). These terms mirror accumulation's Ice (SC Low) and Creek (AR High) — the Ice is at the TOP in distribution (where the CO's supply sits) and the Creek is at the BOTTOM (the last line before markdown).

**A2.** BC mechanics — ATH + extreme volume + upper wick = distribution top, not continuation: The ATH is reached because DEMAND is at its maximum (retail FOMO, momentum buyers all enter simultaneously). This creates the wide range and new high. But the CO is SELLING their entire accumulated position INTO this demand. The upper wick forms because: Buyers drove the intrabar price to the ATH, but as the CO's selling overwhelmed their demand, price fell back from the intrabar high by session end. The close in the lower half confirms the seller (CO) won the session battle. If this were genuine bull continuation, demand would have held price near the intrabar high → close would be in the top third. The upper wick + low close IS the distribution signal.

**A3.** AR volume must be lower than BC volume because the AR is driven by the ABSENCE of demand (buyers who intended to buy just bought everything in the BC). With no new buyers, even modest selling drives price easily lower. A higher-volume AR signals: Selling is MORE intense than the BC's demand was — OR more likely: The BC was only PSY (Preliminary Supply). The genuine distribution BC will occur later, at a higher price, and will be more extreme. A higher-volume AR means the "demand exhaustion" didn't happen at the BC — more markup is likely before the real top forms.

**A4.** UTAD vs Spring comparison: UTAD (distribution) — Price ABOVE the BC High (Ice / range top). Spring (accumulation) — Price BELOW the SC Low (Ice / range bottom). Both: Briefly break the range boundary → Immediately reverse back inside → On the reversal, supply (UTAD) or demand (Spring) is confirmed to be dominant. Key difference: The Spring's LOW volume indicates no real supply was below support. The UTAD's HIGH volume (Type 1) indicates real demand appeared above resistance AND WAS ABSORBED — both serve as confirmation of their respective phase's completion. The Spring occurs at the BOTTOM; the UTAD occurs at the TOP. Both are "the final test before the trend reverses."

**A5.** No Demand test in distribution: Ultra-low volume on a bar near the BC High area, close in the lower half. What ultra-low volume specifically proves: Demand is ABSENT from this level. Buyers who WOULD have bought at prices near the BC High are no longer willing to — the supply they encountered in Phases A/B/C (the UTAD) has absorbed their conviction. Ultra-low volume = almost no trades occurring = nobody wants to buy here at these prices = the demand reservoir is empty. This is the distribution mirror of the accumulation's Positive Test (ultra-low volume near SC Low proves supply is absent there). Both are the most direct, observable confirmation that the absorbing phase is complete.

**A6.** LPSY is optimal short entry because four simultaneous conditions are true: (1) SOW has already CONFIRMED the distribution thesis — Phase D is underway; the range is broken to the downside. Probability of distribution being genuine is at maximum. (2) Volume on the LPSY is LOW — no genuine demand returned to the newly broken support. Directly proves role reversal (old support → new resistance) is complete. (3) Risk is DEFINED — stop is placed above the LPSY High (a specific, measurable level). (4) Price is AT the new resistance (old Creek) — the ratio of remaining downside (full markdown ahead) to upside (LPSY stop) is excellent. All four simultaneously = ideal risk-reward.

**A7.** SOW (Sign of Weakness): Wide down bar, high volume (2–4×), bottom close, breaks BELOW the AR Low (Creek). The Creek (AR Low) was the structural support of the entire distribution range — the floor that held throughout Phase B and C. Breaking below the Creek is the structural announcement that supply has definitively overwhelmed demand and the range has been resolved to the downside. The Creek's break = the same structural significance as the SOS's jump above the Creek in accumulation. Both are the "structural announcement" of phase completion.

**A8.** NSE cash market unsuitable for positional shorts because: NSE cash market requires physical delivery — you cannot sell what you don't own overnight (short-selling in cash = intraday only; you must buy back same day). BTST works for longs; STBT does not work for retail in India's T+1 cash market. For positional Wyckoff distribution shorts: (1) Stock futures — Short futures can be held overnight and rolled across expiry. Margin ~20% of contract value. Best for confirmed LPSY setups. Tradeoff: unlimited loss if wrong; requires margin monitoring. (2) Put options — Buy puts; maximum loss = premium paid. Best when IV is not elevated. Tradeoff: theta decay (lose time value even if right direction but slow); requires choosing strike and expiry carefully.

**A9.** Failed distribution vs genuine UTAD: Genuine UTAD: Price exceeds BC High → Close in the SAME session falls BACK BELOW the BC High → Following session CONTINUES LOWER (or sideways within range, not to new high). Failed distribution (price breaks higher): Price exceeds BC High → Close REMAINS ABOVE the BC High → The NEXT session also closes above the original BC High → Pattern: TWO CONSECUTIVE sessions closing above BC High on volume > 1.5× average. The difference: Genuine UTAD → price cannot SUSTAIN above the BC High even for one full close. Failed distribution → price closes and stays above the BC High, indicating the BC was PSY and the distribution range is re-drawing higher.

**A10.** Five early-warning signals that markdown is ending: (1) Volume divergence on down-legs: Each successive down-wave declines on LESS volume (demand absorbing supply at lower prices). (2) Down-bars narrowing despite high volume: Supply being absorbed (potential SC forming). (3) New low that HOLDS: Price makes a new low but doesn't follow through lower — potential PS. (4) Wide down-bar with LOWER WICK recovery: Price probed the low but recovered → potential SC beginning. (5) CHoCH upward: Price breaks ABOVE a prior Lower High for the first time — structural reversal of the downtrend. Highest importance: #5 — the CHoCH upward. All other signals are WARNINGS; the CHoCH is the STRUCTURAL CONFIRMATION that the markdown is over. Exit all shorts on CHoCH with no exceptions.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS — CHAPTER 14

> **1. The distribution schematic is the exact mirror of accumulation: BC → AR → ST → (UTAD) → SOW → LPSY → Markdown. The Ice is at the TOP (BC High); the Creek is at the BOTTOM (AR Low). The SOW breaks the Creek.**

> **2. The BC looks bullish (ATH + excitement + positive news). The Wyckoff analyst reads beneath the surface: extreme volume + upper wick + close in lower half = professional selling into retail buying. The ATH is the distribution, not the celebration.**

> **3. UTAD = the false breakout above the BC High. Close back below the Ice. Mirror of the Spring. Type 1 (high volume + instant reversal) = highest confidence. Type 2 (low volume) requires No Demand confirmation.**

> **4. SOW = breaks below the Creek (AR Low) on high volume, bottom close. The structural announcement that distribution is complete and markdown begins. Mirror of the SOS in accumulation.**

> **5. LPSY = the optimal short entry: low-volume bounce to the AR Low (now resistance) that fails to sustain, close in lower half. Four simultaneous conditions: SOW confirmed, low volume, holds below Creek, lower half close.**

> **6. NSE short-selling: Cash market = intraday only. Use stock futures for overnight positional shorts. Use ATM put options (3+ weeks expiry) for defined-risk approach. Check margins, lot sizes, expiry before entry.**

> **7. Distribution vs Re-Accumulation: The most commercially critical distinction. Seven criteria — volume at top/bottom, internal structure (LH/LL vs HH/HL), break behaviour, delivery %, relative strength. 5+/7 criteria required for high confidence.**
