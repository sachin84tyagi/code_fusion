## Chapter Overview

If Chapter 8 taught you what volume IS, Chapter 9 teaches you what volume MEANS — specifically, what it means IN RELATIONSHIP TO PRICE.

Price and volume together form a two-variable system. Each variable has two states (high/low, up/down), creating combinations that carry specific analytical weight. But unlike a simple lookup table, these combinations only become actionable when layered with close location, background, and structural context.

This chapter builds the complete analytical framework for reading price-volume combinations — the engine at the heart of VSA, Wyckoff, and all professional volume analysis.

**The Chapter 9 Rule:**

> **Volume tells you HOW HARD the market was working. Price tells you WHAT RESULT it achieved. The relationship between effort and result reveals WHO IS IN CONTROL — and whether that control is strengthening or weakening.**

## LEVEL 1 — BEGINNER

### 9.1 The Eight Core Price-Volume Combinations

![Price-Volume Relationship — The 8 Core Combinations](/images/pi-price-volume-matrix.jpg)

Every bar's volume-price relationship falls into one of these eight fundamental categories. Master these and you have the foundation of all VSA analysis.

---

#### Combination 1: Wide Up Bar + High Volume

```
Price: Large range, close in upper 60%+ of range (bullish bar)
Volume: High (RelVol > 2.0)
```

**Mechanics:** A large number of participants transacted, and buyers collectively pushed price significantly higher. The large range tells you sellers were initially present (price had to sweep through them), but the close location tells you buyers won.

**Interpretation:** Demand dominant. Buyers were aggressive enough to sweep through the available supply at all price levels below the close.

**Context-dependent meaning:**

| Context | Interpretation |
|---------|---------------|
| At support after downtrend | STRONG BULLISH — Demand entering decisively |
| Mid-uptrend (continuation) | BULLISH — Uptrend momentum continuing |
| At resistance in uptrend | CAUTIOUS — May be Buying Climax; check for wick |
| At resistance after extended rally | BEARISH WARNING — Possible BC / Upthrust setup |

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (volume + price direction observed; "buyer dominance" is inferred)

---

#### Combination 2: Wide Down Bar + High Volume

```
Price: Large range, close in lower 40% of range (bearish bar)
Volume: High (RelVol > 2.0)
```

**Mechanics:** Large participation, sellers pushed price significantly lower. The close in the lower portion confirms sellers held control to the end.

**Interpretation:** Supply dominant. Sellers overwhelmed buyers at all price levels above the close.

**Context-dependent meaning:**

| Context | Interpretation |
|---------|---------------|
| At resistance after uptrend | STRONG BEARISH — Supply entering decisively |
| Mid-downtrend (continuation) | BEARISH — Downtrend momentum continuing |
| At support in downtrend | CAUTIOUS — May be Selling Climax; check for lower wick recovery |
| At support after extended decline | BULLISH WARNING — Possible SC; watch next bar |

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE

---

#### Combination 3: Wide Up Bar + Low Volume

```
Price: Large range up, close in upper portion
Volume: Low (RelVol < 0.7)
```

**Mechanics:** Price rose a large amount with very few transactions. This is counterintuitive — it means few sellers were present (so it didn't take much buying to move price).

**Two interpretations (context determines which):**

**Interpretation A — "No Supply" (bullish):**
After a downtrend, if price rises easily on low volume, it means sellers have been exhausted. The market drifts up because nobody is selling. This is a BULLISH background condition.

**Interpretation B — "Weak Rally" (bearish):**
If price rises on low volume in an uptrend NEAR RESISTANCE, it means buyers are not enthusiastic. Few participants are willing to buy at these elevated prices. This can precede a top.

**The differentiator:** Background. Post-downtrend + low volume up = "No Supply." Extended uptrend near resistance + low volume up = "Weak Rally."

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (highly context-dependent)

---

#### Combination 4: Wide Down Bar + Low Volume

```
Price: Large range down, close in lower portion
Volume: Low (RelVol < 0.7)
```

**Two interpretations:**

**Interpretation A — "No Demand" (bearish):**
After an uptrend, price falls easily on low volume = buyers have been exhausted. Few willing to support the market. BEARISH background condition.

**Interpretation B — "Weak Decline" (bullish):**
After an extended downtrend, price falls on very low volume = selling exhaustion. Sellers cannot produce a meaningful decline even on the few shares trading. Precedes "No Supply" bars.

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (highly context-dependent)

---

#### Combination 5: Narrow Bar + High Volume

```
Price: Small range, close near middle (or any close location)
Volume: High (RelVol > 2.0)
```

**Mechanics:** Enormous activity produced almost no price movement. This is the clearest signal of ABSORPTION — two large opposing forces are in balance.

**Interpretation:** One large participant is absorbing the flow from the other:
- At support: Large buyer absorbing seller flow (demand absorbing supply)
- At resistance: Large seller absorbing buyer flow (supply absorbing demand)
- The narrow range is the "fingerprint" of the absorbing order — it prevents price from moving

**This is the most unambiguous volume-price signal in VSA** — because there is no other explanation for massive activity with no price result except absorption.

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE (the only alternative — thin book with mechanical matching — would show even lower volume, not high volume)

---

#### Combination 6: Narrow Bar + Low Volume

```
Price: Small range, close typically in upper portion (for bullish read)
Volume: Low (RelVol < 0.5–0.7)
```

**Mechanics:** Almost no activity and almost no price movement. Neither buyers nor sellers were interested in transacting at this level.

**Two interpretations:**

**"No Supply" (bullish):** If price is at or above a support zone and a low-volume narrow bar appears, it means sellers are absent. Nobody wants to sell here. Price stability on low volume = buyers don't NEED high volume to hold this level — there's no supply pressure.

**"No Demand" (bearish):** If this appears near resistance or in a downtrend, low volume + narrow range = buyers absent. No one is willing to buy at this level.

**The close location tells you which:**
- Close in upper 50%+ = "No Supply" inference
- Close in lower 50%- = "No Demand" inference

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE

---

#### Combination 7: Long Lower Wick + High Volume

```
Price: Wick extends significantly below the body/close; close recovers to upper portion
Volume: High (RelVol > 2.0)
```

**Mechanics:** Price drove aggressively lower (high volume = many sellers), but demand came in strongly at the low and recovered price before the close. The wick is the evidence of the sellers' attack; the recovery close is the evidence of buyers' absorption.

**Interpretation:** Stopping Volume or Selling Climax (Wyckoff). The low of the wick marks a zone of significant demand. High volume confirms broad participation in the recovery.

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE at support; context dependent elsewhere

---

#### Combination 8: Long Upper Wick + High Volume

```
Price: Wick extends significantly above the body/close; close falls back to lower portion
Volume: High (RelVol > 2.0)
```

**Mechanics:** Price drove aggressively higher (high volume = many buyers), but supply came in at the high and forced price back down. The wick = buyers' attempt; the falling close = supply absorption at the high.

**Interpretation:** Upthrust or Buying Climax. The high of the wick marks a zone of significant supply. High volume confirms broad participation in the rejection.

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE at resistance; context dependent elsewhere

### 9.2 The Effort-Result Principle — The Core of VSA

The **Effort-Result principle** is the foundational concept of Volume Spread Analysis:

> **Volume = Effort. Price result = Result. VSA asks: Is the effort proportionate to the result?**

**When effort IS proportionate to result:**
- High volume → Large price movement = Normal. The force is working.
- Low volume → Small price movement = Normal. Little force, little movement.

**When effort is NOT proportionate to result:**
- High volume → Tiny price movement = Anomaly. The effort is being absorbed.
- Low volume → Large price movement = Anomaly. Price moving without effort.

Both anomalies are signals — they indicate something unusual is happening in the order flow.

**The four anomaly types:**

```
Anomaly 1: High effort → No result (Narrow bar, high volume)
Cause: Large opposing order absorbing all flow at this level
Signal: Absorption — note the LOCATION to determine direction

Anomaly 2: Low effort → Large result (Wide bar, low volume)
Cause: No opposing orders at these prices — thin book
Signal: "No Supply" (up) or "No Demand" (down) — exhaustion of the prior trend
Context: Only meaningful after an extended trend

Anomaly 3: Declining effort → Declining result (wave analysis)
Cause: Supply or demand exhausting across multiple waves
Signal: Trend losing momentum — prepare for reversal

Anomaly 4: Same effort → Different result (comparison across timeframe)
Cause: Structural change — same volume producing different price outcomes
Signal: Background condition has changed (absorbed supply, accumulated position)
```

### 9.3 Reading Volume on Up Days vs Down Days

A simple but powerful analysis:

**Within an uptrend — healthy pattern:**
```
Up days: Volume ≥ 1.2× the down-day volume
Down days: Volume ≤ 0.8× the up-day volume

This confirms: Demand is active (volume required to push up)
              Supply is limited (little resistance on pullbacks)
```

**Practical calculation:**

```
Over the last 10 sessions:
Up days: 5 sessions, average volume = 14 lakh shares
Down days: 5 sessions, average volume = 7 lakh shares

Ratio: Up/Down = 14/7 = 2.0 → Healthy uptrend
```

**Deteriorating pattern:**
```
Up days: Average volume = 10 lakh shares (declining)
Down days: Average volume = 9 lakh shares (rising)

Ratio: 10/9 = 1.1 → Warning: Supply growing relative to demand
```

**Reversal confirmation:**
```
Up days: Average volume = 7 lakh shares
Down days: Average volume = 14 lakh shares

Ratio: 7/14 = 0.5 → Supply dominant: Distribution in progress
```

This single metric — the ratio of up-day volume to down-day volume — is one of the most reliable early warning indicators in volume analysis.

### 9.4 Effort-Result Divergence — Waves of Supply and Demand

![Effort vs Result — Volume-Price Divergence Patterns](/images/pi-effort-result-divergence.jpg)

**Wave analysis** reads the effort-result relationship not on single bars but across complete price waves (from swing low to swing high, or vice versa).

**How to perform wave analysis:**

For each complete wave, record:
1. Price distance covered (High − Low of the wave)
2. Total volume during the wave
3. Average volume per day during the wave (if different durations)
4. Duration (number of sessions)

Then compare consecutive waves of the SAME direction.

**Bullish divergence — supply exhausting:**

```
Wave analysis of a downtrend:
Down Wave 1: Price −₹28, Total Volume = 180 lakh, 6 sessions
Down Wave 2: Price −₹22, Total Volume = 140 lakh, 5 sessions
Down Wave 3: Price −₹14, Total Volume = 85 lakh, 4 sessions
Down Wave 4: Price −₹7, Total Volume = 45 lakh, 3 sessions

Each subsequent down wave:
→ Covers less price distance (diminishing result)
→ Uses less volume (diminishing effort)

INFERENCE: Sellers are exhausting. The downtrend is losing both
effort AND result. Supply is running out.
NEXT EVENT WATCH: Selling Climax or Spring
```

**Bearish divergence — demand fading:**

```
Wave analysis of an uptrend:
Up Wave 1: Price +₹30, Total Volume = 220 lakh, 7 sessions
Up Wave 2: Price +₹24, Total Volume = 165 lakh, 6 sessions
Up Wave 3: Price +₹16, Total Volume = 110 lakh, 5 sessions
Up Wave 4: Price +₹9, Total Volume = 60 lakh, 4 sessions

Each subsequent up wave:
→ Covers less price distance (diminishing result)
→ Uses less volume (diminishing effort)

INFERENCE: Buyers are exhausting. The uptrend is losing both
effort AND result. Demand is running out.
NEXT EVENT WATCH: Buying Climax or Upthrust
```

**The critical distinction — same result, different effort:**

```
Bullish case (supply exhausting):
Wave 3 down: Price −₹14, Volume 85 lakh
Wave 4 down: Price −₹14, Volume 30 lakh (SAME result, LESS effort)

This is the most bullish divergence: the market is trying to go down
but needs far less supply to do so. Supply is nearly gone.
```

```
Bearish case (demand fading):
Wave 3 up: Price +₹16, Volume 110 lakh
Wave 4 up: Price +₹16, Volume 45 lakh (SAME result, LESS effort)

The market is reaching the same price level but with far fewer buyers.
This is a more serious bearish divergence.
```

### 9.5 The "Test" — Confirming No Supply or No Demand

A **Test** is a specific price-volume combination that confirms whether supply or demand has dried up at a prior significant level.

**Test for No Supply (bullish):**

```
Context: Price has previously shown a Selling Climax or Stopping Volume
         at a support level. Accumulation may be occurring.

A Test occurs when:
1. Price drops BACK toward the prior SC/support level
2. Volume is VERY LOW (RelVol < 0.5)
3. The bar range is NARROW
4. Price bounces back — closes in UPPER portion of the bar's range

What this tells you:
→ Price revisited the area where supply was heavy before
→ But NOW volume is ultra-low = almost no sellers at this level
→ The supply that previously existed HAS BEEN ABSORBED
→ Nobody is willing to sell at these prices anymore

This is the single most reliable bullish signal in VSA
(when it follows a genuine SC and occurs at the right level)
```

**Test criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Prior SC or Stopping Volume at or near the test level
- [ ] Test bar's Low reaches toward (or slightly into) the SC level
- [ ] Volume ultra-low (RelVol < 0.5, ideally the lowest in 20 sessions)
- [ ] Bar range narrow
- [ ] Close in upper half of the bar (recovery from the probe)
- [ ] Subsequent bar(s) continue upward

All six criteria produce a HIGH-CONFIDENCE test. Fewer criteria = lower confidence.

**Test for No Demand (bearish):**

```
Context: Price has previously shown a Buying Climax or Upthrust
         at a resistance level. Distribution may be occurring.

A Test occurs when:
1. Price rallies BACK toward the prior BC/resistance level
2. Volume is VERY LOW (RelVol < 0.5)
3. Bar range is NARROW
4. Price stalls and closes in LOWER portion of the bar's range

What this tells you:
→ Price revisited the area where demand was heavy before
→ But NOW volume is ultra-low = almost no buyers at this level
→ The demand that previously existed HAS BEEN ABSORBED
→ Nobody is willing to buy at these prices anymore

Bearish signal — confirms distribution at the top
```

### 9.6 Volume Confirmation of Breakouts

A structural breakout (price moving above resistance or below support) must be confirmed by volume. Without volume confirmation, a breakout is suspect.

**Genuine breakout — volume requirements:**

```
Breakout bar:
→ Volume: RelVol > 2.0 (at minimum), ideally > 3.0
→ Range: Wide (price moves decisively through the level)
→ Close: In the top 70%+ of the bar's range (above the broken level)
→ Delivery %: Elevated (> 50–55%)

Post-breakout (next 2–3 sessions):
→ Volume: Remains elevated (not a one-bar wonder)
→ Price: Holds above the broken level
→ Pullback to the level: Occurs on LOWER volume (not panic selling)
```

**Failed breakout — the Upthrust / Spring:**

```
Breakout bar characteristics that WARN of failure:
→ Volume: Very high (climactic — > 5×) but close BACK BELOW the level
→ OR: Very low volume — the break is on thin air, not genuine demand
→ Range: Wide but close in LOWER half (sellers took over by session end)
→ Delivery %: Low (intraday — not committed positions)

Post-break warning signs:
→ Volume collapses the next session (no follow-through)
→ Price immediately returns inside the range
→ No sustained price action above the breakout level
```

**Volume confirmation table:**

| Feature | Genuine Breakout | Failed Breakout (Upthrust) |
|---------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Volume on break bar | High (2–4×) | Very high (5+×) OR very low |
| Close on break bar | Above broken level | Closes BACK below level |
| Volume next 3 sessions | Remains elevated | Collapses |
| Price holds new level | Yes (pullback → holds) | No (returns inside range) |
| Delivery % | Elevated | Low (intraday) |

### 9.7 The No-Supply / No-Demand Background Condition

**No Supply** and **No Demand** are BACKGROUND CONDITIONS, not single-bar signals. They are established by a sequence of bars, not one bar alone.

**No Supply Background (bullish):**

```
Required sequence:
1. Prior downtrend (supply was dominant)
2. A Stopping Volume or SC event (demand absorbed supply climactically)
3. A series of narrow-range bars with declining volume
4. These bars close in the UPPER portion of their ranges
5. Price is stable or drifting up slightly despite no real buying pressure

This sequence tells the story:
→ Supply was heavy (Step 1)
→ Supply was absorbed (Step 2)
→ Now supply is gone — price is stable because no one is selling (Steps 3-5)
→ INFERENCE: When demand returns (even mildly), price will rise easily

The "No Supply" background makes ANY future bullish trigger (SOS, BOS, Test)
much more significant and reliable.
```

**No Demand Background (bearish):**

```
Required sequence:
1. Prior uptrend (demand was dominant)
2. A Buying Climax or Upthrust event (supply absorbed demand climactically)
3. A series of narrow-range bars with declining volume
4. These bars close in the LOWER portion of their ranges
5. Price stable or drifting down despite no real selling pressure

Story:
→ Demand was strong (Step 1)
→ Demand was absorbed at the top (Step 2)
→ Now demand is gone — price is weakening because no one is buying (Steps 3-5)
→ INFERENCE: When supply returns (even mildly), price will fall easily

The "No Demand" background makes ANY future bearish trigger (SOW, CHoCH)
much more significant.
```

## LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED / PROFESSIONAL

### 9.8 Multi-Session Price-Volume Narratives

At the professional level, individual bars are not read in isolation. They are chapters in a story. Here is how to read a complete multi-session price-volume narrative:

**The Accumulation Narrative (8-stage price-volume story):**

```
Stage 1 — Downtrend (Selling pressure):
Bars: Wide down bars with declining volume
Story: Supply was dominant but is weakening. Sellers ran out.

Stage 2 — Selling Climax (Climactic supply):
Bars: Wide down bar, extreme volume (6–10×), lower wick, close recovers
Story: Final panic selling met by large buyer. All remaining supply absorbed.

Stage 3 — Automatic Rally (First demand signal):
Bars: Moderate up bars, volume declining (no supply to overcome)
Story: With supply exhausted, any buying drives price up easily.

Stage 4 — Secondary Test (Supply check):
Bars: Moderate down bar retesting SC zone, volume LESS than SC
Story: Market tests whether supply has returned. Low volume = supply absent.

Stage 5 — Range consolidation (Building the cause):
Bars: Mixed bars, declining overall volume, narrow ranges dominating
Story: Range-bound. Accumulator quietly absorbing whatever supply appears.

Stage 6 — Spring/Shakeout (Final supply test):
Bars: Narrow-low volume bar dips below support briefly, recovers rapidly
Story: Final test — are any sellers left? Low volume = no. Positive test.

Stage 7 — Sign of Strength (Demand emerges):
Bars: Wide up bars, volume expanding, close near highs
Story: Demand takes over. BOS above accumulation range. SOS confirmed.

Stage 8 — Last Point of Support (Markup begins):
Bars: Pullback on low volume to range top (now support), bounces
Story: Final check — will price hold the old resistance? Yes. Markup begins.
```

**Each stage requires specific volume-price evidence. The complete narrative is only confirmed when ALL stages align.**

### 9.9 VWAP and Price-Volume Relationship

**VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price)** is the average price weighted by volume for each trade. It is the most important intraday reference level.

```
VWAP = Σ(Price × Volume) / Σ(Volume)

For each trade: multiply the price by the number of shares traded
Sum all those values, then divide by total volume
```

**Why VWAP matters for price-volume analysis:**

1. **Institutional benchmark:** Most institutional execution algorithms target VWAP. A buyer executing a VWAP order will buy throughout the day, ensuring their average cost ≈ VWAP. This means:
   - Significant institutional buying: Lots of volume near VWAP, price supported at VWAP during pullbacks
   - VWAP becomes a live support/resistance during the session

2. **Price above VWAP:** The "average buyer" is profitable. Bullish sessions tend to close above VWAP.

3. **Price below VWAP:** The "average buyer" is underwater. Bearish sessions tend to close below VWAP.

4. **VWAP rejection:** When price approaches VWAP from below and reverses — sellers defending the average cost.

**NSE-specific VWAP usage:**

On NSE, VWAP data is available for daily sessions. The NSE bhavcopy includes VWAP alongside OHLCV. For Nifty futures, VWAP is calculated from the futures price (not the spot index). Most institutional desks use anchored VWAP (from significant price events like breakouts, gap days, or major swing points) rather than daily VWAP alone.

### 9.10 Quantifying Price-Volume Relationships

**Price Efficiency Ratio (PER):**

```
PER = Price Change / Volume

A higher PER means price moved more per unit of volume (more efficient).
A lower PER means price moved less per unit of volume (less efficient = absorption).

Example:
Session A: Price +₹12, Volume 15 lakh = PER = 0.80 ₹/lakh shares
Session B: Price +₹3, Volume 18 lakh = PER = 0.17 ₹/lakh shares

Session B has very low PER — the large volume produced little price movement.
Absorption inference is strong.
```

**Volume Momentum:**

```
Volume Momentum = Today's Volume / 5-period SMA of Volume

> 1.5: Volume accelerating (expanding momentum)
< 0.7: Volume decelerating (contracting momentum)

Reading: If price is making new highs AND volume momentum > 1.5 = Healthy
         If price is making new highs AND volume momentum < 0.7 = Divergence warning
```

**The VSA Score (composite framework):**

For professional systematic analysis, each bar can be scored:

```
Bar Score = (RelVol Score) + (Close Location Score) + (Range Score) + (Context Score)

RelVol Score:
< 0.5: −2 (No participation)
0.5–1.5: 0 (Neutral)
1.5–3.0: +1 (Elevated)
> 3.0: +2 (High/Climactic)

Close Location Score (adjust sign for direction):
Top 20%: +2
Upper third: +1
Middle: 0
Lower third: −1
Bottom 20%: −2

Range Score:
> 2× ATR: +2 (Wide)
1–2× ATR: +1 (Normal)
< 0.5× ATR: −1 (Narrow — possible absorption)

Context Score:
At support in uptrend: +2
Mid-range: 0
At resistance in uptrend: −1
At resistance in downtrend: −2
At support in downtrend: +1

Total Score:
> +5: Strong bullish bar
+2 to +5: Moderately bullish
−2 to +2: Neutral
−5 to −2: Moderately bearish
< −5: Strong bearish bar
```

This quantified approach allows systematic scanning of watchlists for high-score bars.

## QUIZ ANSWERS

### Conceptual Answers

**A1.** Effort = volume; Result = price movement. High effort → low result: A high-volume bar with narrow range and middle close. Indicates absorption — a large opposing participant is preventing price from moving. Signals a significant player at that level taking the other side of all incoming flow. Low effort → large result: A low-volume bar with wide range. Indicates absence of opposition — price moves easily because no one is standing in its way. Signals exhaustion of the prior trend (no supply or no demand).

**A2.** A narrow-range, high-volume bar definitively tells you: (1) A very large number of shares traded (high participation). (2) Despite all that activity, price barely moved (opposing force at this level). It does NOT tell you: the direction of the opposing force (whether a buyer absorbing sellers or a seller absorbing buyers). Context (location) is required: at support = demand absorbing supply (bullish); at resistance = supply absorbing demand (bearish); mid-range = requires background context.

**A3.** A Test = a low-volume probe of a prior SC/resistance level that finds no supply. Six criteria: (1) Prior SC or Stopping Volume at/near this level. (2) Bar's Low reaches toward the SC level. (3) Volume ultra-low (RelVol < 0.5). (4) Bar range narrow. (5) Close in upper half of the bar. (6) Subsequent bar(s) move higher. Volume is the most important criterion because it directly measures whether supply has returned. If volume were high on the test, supply IS present and the SC was not a genuine bottom.

**A4.** "No Supply" single bar: One narrow-range, low-volume bar closing in the upper half — a single observation that supply was absent in ONE session. "No Supply" background: A sequence of 3–7+ consecutive sessions where volume progressively declines, ranges compress, closes are in upper halves, and price stabilises. The background condition is more reliable because: (1) It represents multiple sessions of supply absence, not just one; (2) It confirms the pattern is sustained, not a random thin-market day; (3) It provides the "storehouse" context that any subsequent demand trigger will find no resistance.

**A5.** Wide up bar on LOW volume is suspicious because: Normally, a large price move requires many transactions (many people buying to sweep through many levels of supply). Low volume means either: (A) No supply existed at those levels — price glided up effortlessly because no sellers were present ("No Supply" — bullish if after a downtrend). (B) Thin market / no genuine buyers — price moved without real participation, suggesting a momentary thin-market artifact or the tail end of a dying rally ("Weak Rally" — bearish near tops). Determinator: Background. Post-SC/downtrend bottom = A. Extended uptrend near resistance = B.

**A6.** Declining volume on successive up-waves = each rally requires fewer participants. Two readings: (1) Bearish (most common interpretation): demand is fading — each new high attracts fewer buyers. The uptrend is running out of fuel. (2) Bullish (less common, less understood): supply has already been absorbed — the market doesn't NEED many buyers to push higher because there are almost no sellers. Determining factor: compare with BEAR waves. If bear-wave volume is also declining = both sides exhausting = transition. If bear-wave volume is rising while bull-wave volume falls = supply growing while demand fades = clearly bearish distribution.

**A7.** Five criteria: (1) Volume (2–4× average), (2) Close well above the broken level (not just barely), (3) Volume sustained in next 2–3 sessions (not one-bar wonder), (4) Price holds above the broken level on pullbacks, (5) Delivery % elevated (committed positions). Most important: the CLOSE of the breakout bar relative to the broken level. If price closes back BELOW the resistance on the breakout bar — regardless of intrabar high — the breakout has already failed (Upthrust).

**A8.** Up-day/Down-day ratio measures whether buyers or sellers are more active per session. Healthy uptrend: Up-day volume ≥ 1.5–2.0× Down-day volume (demand dominant). Deterioration warning: Ratio approaches 1.0 (buyers and sellers equally active). Reversal signal: Ratio < 1.0 (down-day volume exceeds up-day volume = supply dominant). It is a leading indicator because volume patterns typically change BEFORE price structure changes — the ratio deteriorates while price is still making higher highs, giving early warning.

**A9.** VWAP = Volume-Weighted Average Price = Σ(Price × Volume) / Σ(Volume). Institutional participants use it as an execution benchmark — their algorithms target VWAP to minimise market impact and prove best execution compliance. VWAP informs VSA: (1) Price consistently above VWAP = institutional buyers active throughout the day (VWAP algo bid support). (2) Price drops below VWAP = average buyer underwater, may trigger further selling. (3) Volume spikes when price touches VWAP = institutional activity at their benchmark = meaningful level.

**A10.** An SOS volume of only 1.4× is below the ideal 2.0–3.0× threshold. This reduces confidence because: the BOS above the range lacked broad participation. The accumulation thesis is weakened — it may be a false signal or a thin-book breakout. Additional confirmation: (1) The pullback after the SOS (the LPS) should occur on ultra-low volume and hold above the old range top (now support). If it holds on low volume = "No Supply" at the new support = confirms accumulation. (2) A second SOS attempt — if it comes on higher volume = the first low-volume SOS was an early test, the second is the genuine move. (3) Delivery % on the SOS bar — if delivery was elevated despite modest total volume = committed buyers, not HFT noise.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS — CHAPTER 9

> **1. The 8 core price-volume combinations are the vocabulary of VSA. Master them. Each has a specific mechanics-based interpretation that requires context to become actionable.**

> **2. Volume = Effort. Price = Result. High effort + low result = Absorption. Low effort + large result = Exhaustion. Both are anomalies pointing to significant market mechanics.**

> **3. Wave analysis is more reliable than single-bar analysis. Compare successive waves of the SAME direction for effort-result changes across time — this reveals the true momentum of supply and demand.**

> **4. The Test is the single most reliable bullish (or bearish) VSA signal — but only when all six criteria are met. Ultra-low volume on the test bar is the key criterion.**

> **5. "No Supply" and "No Demand" are background conditions built by sequences of bars, not single-bar signals. The background must be established before any trigger can be reliably acted upon.**

> **6. Breakout confirmation requires: High RelVol (2–4×), close above the broken level, sustained volume over 2–3 sessions, and elevated delivery %. A close back below the breakout level on the breakout bar itself = immediate failure (Upthrust).**

> **7. The Up-Day/Down-Day volume ratio is a leading indicator: ratio > 1.5 = healthy uptrend; ratio < 1.0 = supply dominant = distribution likely. This ratio often changes before price structure changes.**
