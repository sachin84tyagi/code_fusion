## Chapter Overview

Chapter 10 gave you the framework — the three laws, four variables, five questions. Chapter 11 delivers the complete pattern library: every named VSA event, precisely defined by its four-variable signature, mechanically explained, and contextually situated within the Wyckoff accumulation/distribution cycle.

There are 15 core VSA patterns. Twelve appear in the Wyckoff cycle (six bullish/accumulation, six bearish/distribution) and three are standalone background conditions (No Supply, No Demand, Test). Every pattern is presented with:
1. **Four-variable definition** (Spread / Volume / Close / Background required)
2. **Mechanical explanation** (WHY this combination occurs)
3. **Criteria checklist** (minimum requirements for the pattern to be valid)
4. **Evidence classification** (OBSERVATION/INFERENCE/HYPOTHESIS)
5. **Confirmation and invalidation** rules

**The Chapter 11 Rule:**

> **A pattern name is a label for a specific four-variable combination in a specific context. Never work backwards from the name to the chart. Always work forward: read the four variables → determine the context → apply the label last, if applicable.**

## LEVEL 1 — BEGINNER: THE COMPLETE BULLISH PATTERN LIBRARY

### 11.1 Preliminary Support (PS)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase A — first sign of demand in a downtrend.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Moderate to wide (down bar)
- Volume: Elevated but NOT climactic (1.5–3× average)
- Close: Middle to upper half of range (partial recovery)
- Background: Extended downtrend — multiple prior down-bars on declining volume

**Mechanics:**

The downtrend has been running. Volume on each down-wave has been declining. Suddenly, on one bar, selling meets significant buying interest. The bar's range is wide (selling existed) but the close recovers partially (demand appeared). Volume is elevated but not extreme — this is the FIRST significant buying interest, not the final capitulation.

**Practical identification:**

```
What you see on the chart:
- Extended downtrend preceding this bar
- Wider range than recent bars
- Volume higher than recent bars but NOT a spike
- Price closes noticeably better than the low
- May appear as a long lower wick with partial recovery

What this means:
- The first meaningful demand is appearing
- Sellers still control (close not in top third)
- But buyers are beginning to SLOW the decline
- This is NOT a buy signal — it is an OBSERVATION that the downtrend may be tiring
```

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Prior downtrend of meaningful duration (at least 10–15 bars)
- [ ] Prior volume was declining on down-bars
- [ ] This bar's volume is elevated relative to recent bars
- [ ] Close is in the upper 40%+ of the bar's range
- [ ] Price does NOT immediately continue sharply lower next session

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (preliminary demand appearing)

**Action:** Watch. Do NOT buy. Preliminary Support merely signals that demand is starting to appear. Much more evidence needed.

### 11.2 Selling Climax (SC)

![VSA Bullish Pattern Library — Accumulation Side](/images/pi-vsa-bullish-patterns.jpg)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase A — the bottom event.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Very wide (largest or near-largest bar in the downtrend)
- Volume: Climactic (typically ≥ 5–8× recent average, often the highest in weeks/months)
- Close: Middle to upper half of range (significant lower wick recovery)
- Background: Extended downtrend, PS may have occurred earlier, volume declining on prior bars

**Mechanics:**

The SC is the moment of maximum fear. Every remaining retail holder who intended to sell finally panics. The cascade of SL-M orders and panic selling creates:
1. Extreme volume (all the remaining supply hits the market simultaneously)
2. Very wide range (market impact sweeps through thin bid side)
3. A lower wick recovery (professional demand absorbs the panic selling at the low)

The professional buyer who has been waiting at the bottom absorbs every panicked sell. By session end, price has recovered substantially from the intrabar low — this recovery IS the absorption.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Volume is the highest (or near-highest) of the prior 20–40 sessions
- [ ] Range is the widest (or near-widest) of the prior 20–40 sessions
- [ ] A lower wick is visible (price probed lower but recovered)
- [ ] Close is in the upper 30%+ of the bar's range (significant recovery)
- [ ] Prior trend: Extended downtrend (LH/LL sequence)
- [ ] Volume on prior bars was declining (selling was exhausting)
- [ ] Price is at a significant support level (prior swing low, round number, long-term support)

**NSE specifics for SC identification:**

```
Delivery % check: On the SC bar, delivery should be ELEVATED (> 45–55%)
→ High delivery = positional buyers absorbed the panic selling
→ Low delivery = mostly intraday traders; less conviction in the reversal

FII/DII data check: Did FIIs or DIIs show net buying on this session?
→ Institutional net buying confirms professional demand absorbed retail selling
```

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE when all criteria met

**Action:** Do NOT buy on the SC bar itself. It is an OBSERVATION of potential climax. The next 3–5 bars will confirm or deny.

**Common mistake:** Buying the SC bar thinking it's a bottom. The SC defines the POTENTIAL bottom — confirmation comes from the AR and especially the ST.

### 11.3 Automatic Rally (AR)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase A — immediately follows the SC.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Moderate to wide (up bars)
- Volume: Declining relative to the SC (typically 1.5–2.5×, LESS than SC)
- Close: Upper half of bars
- Background: Immediately follows the SC bar

**Mechanics:**

After the SC exhausts selling, even modest buying drives price up rapidly because:
1. Supply is temporarily exhausted (sellers just sold)
2. Short sellers who sold into the SC are nervous and begin covering (buying)
3. The buyers who absorbed the SC now have no competing sellers

The AR defines the TOP of the accumulation range. The range's height (SC low to AR high) and width (SC to eventual SOS) determines the size of the subsequent markup.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Follows within 1–5 sessions of the SC
- [ ] Volume is LOWER than the SC volume (crucial — if higher, the SC wasn't genuine)
- [ ] Price rises 5–15% from the SC low (range varies by stock)
- [ ] Multiple up-bars in sequence on declining but still-above-average volume
- [ ] Stops well below prior significant resistance

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (confirms SC was likely genuine)

**Volume rule:** The AR's peak volume must be less than the SC's volume. If the AR volume exceeds the SC, the SC may have been a continuation bar (more selling to come), not a genuine climax.

### 11.4 Secondary Test (ST)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase B — testing the SC level.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow to moderate (declining range on the test bar)
- Volume: LOW (often the lowest of the prior 10–20 sessions)
- Close: Upper half of the test bar (holds above SC low, close recovers)
- Background: Follows the AR; price pulls back toward the SC level

**Mechanics:**

The market "tests" whether supply has returned to the SC area. If supply is truly exhausted, the retest will show very low volume — nobody wants to sell at this level because the sellers from the SC already exited. The low volume IS the evidence that supply has been absorbed.

```
Successful ST (Bullish):
- Price approaches SC level
- Volume dries up completely (often 0.3–0.5× average)
- Price holds ABOVE the SC low (or very close to it)
- Close in upper half of the test bar
- Next session: price moves away from the test area

Failed ST (Bearish — SC was not genuine):
- Price approaches SC level
- Volume is high on the retest (sellers ARE present)
- Price breaches the SC low significantly
- Close in lower half or near new low
- Suggests the SC was not a genuine bottom; more decline ahead
```

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Occurs within 5–15 sessions of the SC
- [ ] Volume on the test bar is LOWER than SC volume (ideally < 0.5×)
- [ ] Low of the test bar is at or above the SC low (or very small breach)
- [ ] Close in upper half of the test bar
- [ ] Range is narrow relative to the SC bar

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE when criteria met — the lowest volume on the ST is the most important single criterion in all of VSA.

### 11.5 Spring (Shakeout)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase C — the final supply test.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow to moderate (the spring bar itself); the dip below support is the key event
- Volume: LOW (critical — high volume Spring is a different animal)
- Close: Strong recovery — upper 50%+ of range, back ABOVE the support level
- Background: Accumulation range established (SC, AR, ST occurred); price is near the range bottom

**Mechanics:**

The Spring is the "false breakdown." Price dips BELOW the established support of the accumulation range (the SC low area or range bottom), triggering:
1. Long stops placed by range traders below the support
2. Short entries by breakout traders shorting the "breakdown"

But then price IMMEDIATELY reverses back above the broken support. The Spring reveals two things:
1. The supply below support was thin (low volume = few sellers actually materialized)
2. The buyers absorbed all selling instantly and drove price back up
3. The weak holders (those who placed stops just below support) have now been flushed out

After the Spring, the accumulation is typically complete. The remaining holders are committed (their stops have been taken out or they held through). The stage is set for markup.

**Two types of Spring:**

```
Type 1 Spring (Classic):
- Volume LOW on the dip below support
- This is the ideal Spring — confirms no real supply below support
- High confidence bullish inference

Type 2 Spring (Stopping Volume / High Volume Spring):
- Volume HIGH on the dip below support
- Supply IS present but is being ABSORBED by strong demand
- Price still recovers rapidly
- Less ideal but still valid — the high volume tells you there was a real fight
- Requires additional confirmation (positive Test after the Spring)
```

**Spring criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Accumulation range established (SC, AR, and at least one ST present)
- [ ] Price dips below the range support/SC low area
- [ ] Volume LOW on the Spring (ideally < 0.7× average for Type 1)
- [ ] Recovery is FAST — same session or next session closes back above support
- [ ] Close in upper half of the Spring bar's range
- [ ] The new low is only marginally below the prior support (< 2–3%)

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE for Type 1 Spring; INFERENCE for Type 2 (requires confirmation)

**NSE Spring identification tip:**

On NSE, Springs often occur on F&O expiry weeks when stop-loss orders below well-known support levels are triggered by derivative-driven price action. Check whether a Spring coincides with F&O expiry and whether the volume is expiry-distorted or genuine.

### 11.6 Sign of Strength (SOS) / Jump Across the Creek (JAC)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase D — demand takes structural control.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Wide (up bar — often 1.5–2.5× ATR)
- Volume: High (2–4× average — notably higher than prior range bars)
- Close: Top third (> 70% of bar's range)
- Background: Spring has occurred (or ST has held); accumulation range complete

**Mechanics:**

The SOS is the bar where demand decisively overwhelms supply. Price breaks ABOVE the accumulation range's resistance (the AR high). The high volume confirms broad buyer participation — this is not a thin-air drift up but a genuine buyer-driven breakout. The wide range confirms buyers swept through all available supply at every price level up to the new high.

**The "Creek" metaphor (Wyckoff):**

Wyckoff described the resistance at the top of the accumulation range as the "Creek." The SOS bar "Jumps Across the Creek" — breaking above it decisively.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Accumulation phase complete (SC, AR, ST, Spring all identified)
- [ ] Volume 2–4× the recent average (not a thin-air break)
- [ ] Range wide (> 1.5× ATR)
- [ ] Close in top 70%+ of bar's range
- [ ] Bar closes ABOVE the AR high / accumulation range top
- [ ] Delivery % elevated (> 50–55%)

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE — strongest buy signal in VSA/Wyckoff

---

### 11.7 Last Point of Support (LPS)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Accumulation Phase D–E — pullback after SOS.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow (compression on the pullback)
- Volume: Low (< 0.7× average — no supply on the pullback)
- Close: Upper half (holds above the old AR high / breakout level)
- Background: After SOS; price has pulled back to test the old resistance (now support)

**Mechanics:**

After the SOS, price cannot go up indefinitely without pausing. The LPS is the pullback to the "Creek" level (the old accumulation range top) that now serves as support. The low volume on the LPS tells you: No supply returned to the newly broken level. The old resistance has truly become support. This is the LAST safe point to accumulate a long position before the major markup begins.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Follows SOS within 3–10 sessions
- [ ] Price pulls back toward the broken resistance level (now support)
- [ ] Volume VERY LOW on the pullback (< 0.7×, ideally the lowest since the Spring)
- [ ] Price holds ABOVE the old resistance (the old "Creek")
- [ ] Close in upper half of the LPS bar
- [ ] Subsequent bar moves away from the LPS level upward

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE — optimal entry point in the entire Wyckoff cycle

## THE COMPLETE BEARISH PATTERN LIBRARY

### 11.8 Preliminary Supply (PSY)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Distribution Phase A — first sign of supply in an uptrend.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Moderate to wide (up bar on high volume, but close doesn't hold near the high)
- Volume: Elevated (not climactic yet)
- Close: Middle or lower half (price rose but couldn't hold gains)
- Background: Extended uptrend, volume has been declining on up-bars

**Mechanics:** First significant supply appears in an uptrend. Price makes a new high but immediately gives back ground — sellers appear at the high. Volume elevated but not climactic. This is the mirror image of Preliminary Support.

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (preliminary supply appearing)
**Action:** Watch. Do not short yet.

---

### 11.9 Buying Climax (BC)

![VSA Bearish Pattern Library — Distribution Side](/images/pi-vsa-bearish-patterns.jpg)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Distribution Phase A — the top event.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Very wide (largest or near-largest bar in the uptrend)
- Volume: Climactic (≥ 5–8× recent average)
- Close: Middle to lower half of range (significant upper wick — price fell from the high)
- Background: Extended uptrend, volume has been declining on recent up-waves

**Mechanics:**

The BC is the moment of maximum greed. Retail FOMO buyers all rush in simultaneously. The cascade of market buy orders creates:
1. Extreme volume
2. Wide range (price sweeps through thin ask side)
3. An upper wick (professional distribution absorbs all buying at the high — price falls from the extreme)

The professional seller who has been waiting at the top sells their entire accumulated position into this mass of buying demand. By session end, price has fallen substantially from the intrabar high — this collapse IS the distribution.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Volume highest (or near-highest) of the prior 20–40 sessions
- [ ] Range widest (or near-widest) of the prior 20–40 sessions
- [ ] Upper wick visible (price probed higher but fell back)
- [ ] Close in lower 50% of the bar's range (failed to hold the high)
- [ ] Prior trend: Extended uptrend (HH/HL sequence)
- [ ] Volume on prior up-waves was declining (demand exhausting)
- [ ] Price is at a significant resistance level (prior high, round number, etc.)

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE when all criteria met

**NSE BC check:**
- Delivery % should be LOW (< 30%) — retail FOMO buyers often sell the next day; no conviction
- FII data may show net SELLING on the BC day (confirming institutional distribution into retail buying)

---

### 11.10 Automatic Reaction (AR — Distribution)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Distribution Phase A — immediately follows BC.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Moderate to wide (down bars)
- Volume: Declining relative to BC (LESS than BC volume)
- Close: Lower half of bars
- Background: Immediately follows the BC bar

**Mechanics:** After the BC exhausts buying, even modest selling drives price down. Defines the BOTTOM of the distribution range. Mirror image of the Accumulation AR.

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE

---

### 11.11 Upthrust (UT)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Distribution Phase B–C — false breakout at range top.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Wide (bar reaches new high briefly)
- Volume: Moderate to high
- Close: BACK BELOW the resistance level (the key criterion)
- Background: Distribution range established; price tests the BC high level

**Mechanics:**

The Upthrust is the mirror image of the Spring. Price briefly breaks ABOVE the distribution range's resistance (the BC high), triggering:
1. Breakout buy orders from momentum traders
2. Short-covering from early shorts

But then price IMMEDIATELY falls back below the broken resistance. The rapid reversal tells you: Supply is massive at and above this level. Every buyer is immediately met with a seller who drives price back down.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Distribution range established (BC, AR identified)
- [ ] Price briefly exceeds the BC high (or prior range top)
- [ ] Volume moderate to high on the push above
- [ ] Close BACK BELOW the resistance level (within the range or below)
- [ ] Subsequent session continues lower
- [ ] The new high is only marginally above prior resistance (< 2–3%)

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE at distribution tops

---

### 11.12 Sign of Weakness (SOW) / Ice Break

**Phase:** Wyckoff Distribution Phase D — supply takes structural control.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Wide (down bar)
- Volume: High (2–4× average)
- Close: Bottom third (< 30% of bar's range)
- Background: Distribution range complete; Upthrust has occurred

**Mechanics:**

The SOW is the bar where supply decisively overwhelms demand. Price breaks BELOW the distribution range's support (the AR low). The high volume confirms broad seller participation — a genuine supply-driven breakdown, not a thin-market drift.

Wyckoff's term: "Break through the Ice." The "Ice" is the support at the distribution range bottom.

**Criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Distribution phase complete (BC, AR, UT all identified)
- [ ] Volume 2–4× recent average
- [ ] Range wide (> 1.5× ATR)
- [ ] Close in bottom third of bar's range
- [ ] Bar closes BELOW the AR low / distribution range bottom
- [ ] Delivery % elevated (committed sellers)

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE — strongest sell signal in VSA/Wyckoff

---

### 11.13 Last Point of Supply (LPSY)

**Phase:** Wyckoff Distribution Phase D–E — dead-cat bounce after SOW.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow (low momentum on the bounce)
- Volume: Low (no genuine demand returning)
- Close: Lower half (sellers continue to control)
- Background: After SOW; price bounces weakly toward the old support (now resistance)

**Mechanics:**

After the SOW, a weak bounce (the "LPSY") occurs. This is the last opportunity to short (for those who missed the SOW). Low volume on the bounce = no real buyers. Close in lower half = sellers still in control. The LPSY is the final test of the broken support (now resistance): price approached it and failed, confirming the role reversal.

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE — optimal short entry after SOW

## LEVEL 2 — INTERMEDIATE: THE THREE BACKGROUND CONDITION PATTERNS

### 11.14 No Supply (NS)

**Phase:** Any phase where the prior selling has been absorbed — most common in accumulation.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow to moderate
- Volume: LOW (< 0.5× average, ideally < 0.3×)
- Close: Upper half (> 50%)
- Background: Prior SC or absorption has occurred; accumulation context

**Mechanics:**

No Supply is NOT a reversal signal — it is a BACKGROUND CONDITION confirming that supply has been absorbed. When volume is ultra-low and price is stable or rising gently, it means: sellers are ABSENT. Nobody is willing to sell at this level. This absence of supply sets the stage for easy price appreciation when demand returns.

**The three No Supply criteria:**
1. Volume ultra-low (the fewer sellers, the more meaningful)
2. Close in upper half (buyers hold marginal control even with no sellers)
3. Prior accumulation context (SC/absorption has occurred)

**What it is NOT:**
- A buy signal by itself
- Confirmation of an uptrend without other VSA context
- Valid in a distribution phase (where it might be "No Demand" instead)

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (confirms prior absorption; bullish background only)

**Practical scan criterion for NSE:**

```
Screen for:
- Delivery % declining (fewer participants holding overnight)
- Volume < 40% of 20-day average
- Close in top 50% of bar range
- Price within 2% of a prior SC level or range support
- Pattern persisting for 3–7 sessions in a row

Multiple consecutive No Supply bars = very strong accumulation background
```

---

### 11.15 No Demand (ND)

**Phase:** Any phase where prior buying has been absorbed — most common in distribution.

**Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow to moderate
- Volume: LOW (< 0.5× average)
- Close: Lower half (< 50%), often middle or below
- Background: Prior BC or absorption has occurred; distribution context

**Mechanics:**

No Demand is the mirror of No Supply. Ultra-low volume with a close in the lower half = buyers are absent. Nobody is willing to buy at this level. The absence of demand sets the stage for easy price decline when supply returns.

**The trap:** A No Demand bar can appear during a normal pullback in an uptrend and has NO bearish implication. Context (distribution background) is required for the bearish interpretation.

**Evidence class:** INFERENCE (confirms prior absorption; bearish background only)

---

### 11.16 The Test (Positive and Negative)

**Phase:** Accumulation (positive test = bullish) or Distribution (negative test = bearish).

**Positive Test (Bullish) — Four-Variable Definition:**
- Spread: Narrow
- Volume: ULTRA-LOW (often the lowest RelVol in 20+ sessions)
- Close: Upper half (recovery from the probe low)
- Background: After SC/ST; price retests the SC area or a prior support

**Mechanics of the Positive Test:**

A Test is a deliberate probe of a prior zone to check whether supply has returned. If the market tests the SC area and finds:
- Ultra-low volume → supply is ABSENT
- Close in upper half → marginal demand holds

This confirms the prior absorption is still intact. The "supply reservoir" has not refilled.

**Positive test criteria checklist:**
- [ ] Prior SC or significant support event in background
- [ ] Volume ultra-low (lowest RelVol in recent 20 sessions ideally)
- [ ] Range narrow
- [ ] Close in upper half (> 50%)
- [ ] Does NOT close below the prior SC low

**Negative Test (Bearish):**
The mirror image — a probe of the BC level on ultra-low volume that closes in the lower half. Confirms demand is absent; supply remains dominant.

**Evidence class:** STRONG INFERENCE when volume is ultra-low (the primary variable)

## LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED / PROFESSIONAL

### 11.17 The Complete Pattern Interaction Map

The 15 patterns interact in a specific sequence during Wyckoff phases. Understanding the MAP prevents misidentification.

**Accumulation interaction sequence:**

```
Phase A (Stopping the downtrend):
1. PS → Downtrend slows (OPTIONAL — may not appear)
2. SC → Climactic bottom (REQUIRED)
3. AR → Bounce, defines range top (REQUIRED)
4. ST → Tests SC level (REQUIRED — may occur multiple times)

Phase B (Building the cause):
5. Multiple STs and mini-Springs within the range
6. No Supply bars during range consolidation
7. Delivery % rising quietly

Phase C (Testing supply — last chance for supply to appear):
8. Spring → Dips below range (REQUIRED in classic schematic; absent in some non-classic accumulations)
9. Test of Spring → Ultra-low volume confirms no supply (REQUIRED after Spring)

Phase D (Demand taking control):
10. SOS / JAC → Breaks above AR high (REQUIRED)
11. LPS → Pullback to old resistance on low volume (REQUIRED)

Phase E (Markup):
12. Back-Up to Edge of Creek (BUEC) → One final test of the breakout level
13. Sustained HH/HL markup trend
```

**Distribution interaction sequence:**

```
Phase A: PSY → BC → AR → ST (mirror of accumulation Phase A)
Phase B: Multiple STs and mini-Upthrusts; No Demand bars; delivery % falling
Phase C: UT (Upthrust After Distribution — UTAD) → Tests supply above range
Phase D: SOW → Breaks below AR low; LPSY → Dead-cat bounce on low volume
Phase E: Sustained markdown (LH/LL sequence)
```

### 11.18 Non-Classic Accumulations and Distributions

In practice, the Wyckoff schematic is rarely as clean as the textbook. Common variations:

**Accumulation without a Spring:**

Some accumulations do not produce a Spring — they SOS directly from within the range. This happens when:
- Supply was so thoroughly absorbed during Phase B that no test below support is needed
- The SOS occurs from the middle or upper portion of the accumulation range
- Volume on the SOS is particularly high and delivery very elevated

**Identification:** Multiple successful STs, progressively declining volume, multiple No Supply bars → then SOS. The Spring step is simply skipped when supply was fully absorbed earlier.

**Accumulation with multiple STs:**

The range may have 3–5+ Secondary Tests before the Spring/SOS. Each ST should show:
- Lower volume than the prior ST (supply diminishing progressively)
- Price not closing below the prior ST's close (demand holding)
- The pattern of multiple STs = accumulator building position over a long period

**Failed Spring (continuation down):**

When a Spring occurs on HIGH volume and price does NOT recover:
- High volume below support = supply IS present below the range
- Recovery is weak (close in lower half) or absent
- This is NOT a Spring — it is a genuine breakdown (SOW below the accumulation range)
- The prior SC was not the final bottom; more decline ahead

```
Spring vs Breakdown:
Volume below support: Low = Spring. High = Breakdown.
Close recovery: Strong = Spring. Weak/none = Breakdown.
Next session: Higher = Spring confirmed. Lower = Breakdown confirmed.
```

### 11.19 VSA Pattern Reliability Scoring — NSE Context

Not all patterns are equally reliable. This scoring accounts for NSE market characteristics:

| Pattern | Reliability | Key conditions | NSE caution |
|---------|------------|----------------|------------|
| SC | High | All 6 criteria met | Verify not earnings/bulk deal distorted |
| ST (low volume) | Very High | Volume is lowest in 20 sessions | Most reliable single signal |
| Positive Test | Very High | Ultra-low volume on probe | Check no F&O expiry distortion |
| Spring (Type 1) | High | Low volume, instant recovery | F&O expiry can create fake Springs |
| SOS | High | High volume, top close, breaks range | Ensure not thin-market breakout |
| LPS | High | Low volume pullback holds level | Strong confirmation of SOS |
| No Supply | Moderate | Multiple bars, rising delivery | False positive in thin markets |
| BC | High | All 6 criteria met | Verify not earnings-driven |
| UT | High | Close back below resistance | Confirm with next session |
| SOW | High | High volume, bottom close, breaks | Most reliable after UT |
| LPSY | Moderate | Low volume bounce fails | Confirm with continued lower closes |
| No Demand | Moderate | Multiple bars, falling delivery | False positive mid-trend |
| PS/PSY | Low | Early identification difficult | Use only for context, not signals |

### 11.20 Pattern Misidentification — The Most Common Errors

**Error 1: Calling every high-volume down bar a "Selling Climax"**

An SC requires:
- PRIOR downtrend (not a first drop from recent highs)
- The highest (or near-highest) volume in weeks
- A recovery close (lower wick)
- The bar being at a significant support level

A high-volume down bar mid-trend with a bottom close is a BEARISH continuation bar (SOW in a downtrend), not an SC.

**Error 2: Calling a low-volume up bar "No Supply" without context**

A low-volume up bar is "No Supply" only when:
- Prior accumulation/SC exists in the background
- You are at or above a support level
- The close is in the upper half

A low-volume up bar in a distribution phase near resistance = likely "No Demand" (or the beginning of an Upthrust) — NOT bullish.

**Error 3: Treating the Spring as the buy entry**

The Spring is the TEST — not the entry. The Spring's confirmation (the next session closing above the Spring bar's high on expanding volume) is the signal. Entering ON the Spring bar means buying into uncertainty — you don't yet know if it will recover.

**Error 4: Ignoring failed patterns**

Every pattern can fail. A "failed SC" (price continues lower after the SC) invalidates the accumulation thesis. A "failed Spring" (price cannot recover above support) is a breakdown. The failure pattern is often the most valuable signal — it tells you the prior hypothesis was wrong.

**The discipline of invalidation:**

```
Before entering any trade based on a VSA pattern, define:
"This pattern is invalidated if [specific bar event] occurs."

Example SC trade:
Entry: After ST on ultra-low volume
Stop: Below the SC low (invalidation = SC was not genuine bottom)
Target: AR high (first), then 1× range size above AR high

If price closes below SC low after entry: Exit immediately.
The invalidation event IS the stop trigger — not a number you choose.
```

## QUIZ ANSWERS

### Conceptual Answers

**A1.** Minimum required events for a valid accumulation: (1) SC (mandatory — defines the bottom). (2) AR (mandatory — defines the range top). (3) ST (mandatory — confirms supply is absorbed). Spring is required in the classic schematic but can be absent in non-classic accumulations. SOS is mandatory — it confirms demand has taken control. Most important single confirmation: the ST on ultra-low volume. If the ST occurs at the SC level on LOW volume, it is the single strongest piece of evidence that supply has been absorbed and the SC was genuine.

**A2.** SC close typically in the MIDDLE of the bar's range because: The SC bar represents a battle. Sellers are extremely active (the downtrend's final capitulation creates the wide range). Buyers (professional absorbers) are also active, absorbing all selling. By session end, the buyers have been successful enough to drive price significantly above the intrabar low (lower wick forms) — but NOT so decisively that they push close to the top. The middle close reflects: Sellers were very powerful (drove price to extreme low during session) AND Buyers were powerful enough to absorb it all and recover significantly by close. Neither side was completely dominant — which is exactly what you expect at a genuine equilibrium point that ends a downtrend.

**A3.** Spring vs genuine breakdown — two most important criteria: (1) Volume on the dip below support: LOW = Spring (no supply below); HIGH = Breakdown (supply IS present below). (2) Close recovery: Strong recovery (close well back above broken support) = Spring; Weak or no recovery (close near low or below support) = Breakdown. These two criteria together are decisive: Spring = low volume + strong close. Breakdown = high volume + weak close. One of these two criteria being wrong (e.g., low volume but weak close, or strong close but high volume) creates ambiguity requiring additional confirmation.

**A4.** LPS is the optimal entry because: (1) The SOS (JAC) has already confirmed the Wyckoff thesis — the risk of the accumulation failing is now significantly lower. (2) The LPS pullback occurs on LOW volume — no supply is returning to contest the breakout. This is the cleanest evidence that the old resistance has truly become support. (3) The risk/reward is better than the Spring: stop can be placed just below the breakout level (a well-defined level), target is the full markup move, and entry risk is lower than the Spring's uncertainty. (4) Entering at the Spring means entering when the thesis is INFERENTIAL. Entering at the LPS means entering when the thesis is near-CONFIRMED. Higher probability = appropriate for larger position sizing.

**A5.** Upthrust mechanics: Price breaks above the distribution range's resistance (the BC high). This triggers: (a) Buy-stop orders from breakout traders (placed above the known resistance). (b) Short-covering from early shorts (who placed stops above resistance). This creates buying — which the professional seller exploits by distributing their remaining position INTO this buying. The professional seller is on the SELL side at the UT high; the retail/momentum traders are on the BUY side. After all the buy-stops and short-covering are absorbed, no more demand exists above the resistance. Supply overwhelms, price falls back below resistance. The UT high marks the top of the supply zone.

**A6.** ST volume is the most important criterion because: Ultra-low volume on the ST DIRECTLY PROVES that supply has not returned to the SC area. It is the closest thing to a directly observable fact in VSA: low volume = few trades occurred = few sellers were willing to sell at this level. This is NOT an inference about participant intentions — it is a direct measurement. If supply had returned, the ST would show high volume. The absence of volume is the absence of supply, which is the condition needed for the markup to begin. No other criterion in VSA provides such direct mechanistic confirmation.

**A7.** No Supply: Bullish background condition. Required prior event: SC (or significant absorption at support). Shows supply is absent — price is stable or drifting up because nobody is willing to sell. Sets the stage for easy price appreciation. No Demand: Bearish background condition. Required prior event: BC (or significant absorption at resistance). Shows demand is absent — price is stable or drifting down because nobody is willing to buy. Sets the stage for easy price decline. Without the prior absorptive event, "No Supply" is just a quiet market day, and "No Demand" is just a slow pullback — neither has directional implications.

**A8.** AR volume must be LOWER than SC volume because: The AR represents the first bounce after the SC, driven by absence of supply (sellers exhausted themselves in the SC). If the AR volume were HIGHER than the SC, it would mean: Either (a) demand on the rally is greater than the supply at the SC — possible but unusual; or (b) the SC was not the genuine climactic bottom — more sellers remain. A higher-volume AR often signals that the SC was a Preliminary Support rather than the final SC, and another, more extreme volume event will occur as the true bottom. In practice: AR volume should be 20–40% of SC volume ideally.

**A9.** Non-classic (no Spring) accumulation differences: (1) The SOS must be on particularly high volume and strong delivery — because without the Spring, there was no final "flush out" of weak holders; the SOS must demonstrate overwhelming demand to compensate. (2) No cheap Spring entry — the trader must enter on the SOS or LPS instead. (3) Additional confirmation needed: The LPS must hold with ULTRA-LOW volume (confirming supply truly absent despite no Spring shake-out). (4) Multiple STs with progressively declining volume (confirming supply was absorbed during Phase B without needing a final spring).

**A10.** Without SC, AR, and ST in the background, the "SOS" is NOT a Wyckoff SOS — it is just a wide up bar on high volume that breaks out. Alternative interpretations: (a) A genuine breakout from a valid sideways range that was not an accumulation (but perhaps a reaccumulation mid-trend). (b) A false breakout (Upthrust if it fails). (c) A wide bar from an event (earnings, news). In the absence of a complete accumulation background, the appropriate response is: (1) Apply all four VSA variables. (2) Check structural context (is this a range breakout or an open-air bar?). (3) Wait for the pullback: if price pulls back on low volume and holds (LPS conditions), THEN the breakout is more credible. Without accumulation background, no "SOS" label should be applied — it is simply a "wide up bar on high volume at range top" requiring confirmation.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS — CHAPTER 11

> **1. The complete bullish pattern sequence: PS → SC → AR → ST → Spring → SOS → LPS. Each event progressively confirms the accumulation thesis. Missing events reduce confidence proportionally.**

> **2. The Selling Climax requires: highest volume of prior weeks, widest range, lower wick recovery, close in middle-upper half, at a significant support. It marks the POTENTIAL bottom — not the confirmed bottom.**

> **3. The Secondary Test is the most important single confirmation in VSA. Ultra-low volume on the ST directly proves supply is absent. It is the closest thing to a measurable fact in the entire framework.**

> **4. Spring (Type 1): LOW volume dip below support + instant strong recovery. Spring (Type 2): HIGH volume dip + strong recovery. Type 1 = stronger evidence; Type 2 requires confirmation Test.**

> **5. SOS (Sign of Strength) = wide up bar, high volume, top close, breaks above accumulation range. LPS (Last Point of Support) = narrow, low-volume pullback to old resistance, holds. LPS is the OPTIMAL trade entry.**

> **6. The bearish patterns are exact mirrors: BC → AR → UT → ND → SOW → LPSY. Same mechanics, reversed direction. BC = distribution top; UT = false breakout at top; SOW = structural breakdown.**

> **7. Pattern misidentification errors: Every high-volume down bar is NOT an SC. Every low-volume up bar is NOT No Supply. A Spring on high volume is Type 2, requiring confirmation. Always verify all four variables AND prior background before naming any pattern.**
