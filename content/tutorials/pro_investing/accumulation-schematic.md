## LEVEL 1 — BEGINNER

### 13.1 The Complete Accumulation Schematic

![Wyckoff Accumulation Schematic — Complete Phase Map (Phases A through E)](/images/pi-accumulation-schematic-full.jpg)

The Wyckoff accumulation schematic is a map of how professional money enters the market at low prices over a controlled period of time. It has five phases (A–E), each with a distinct purpose and a distinct set of VSA events.

**The two key reference levels:**

Every accumulation schematic is anchored by two horizontal reference lines:

```
Top Reference — "The Creek" (AR High):
The high of the Automatic Rally after the SC.
This becomes the TOP of the accumulation range.
Price must JUMP this level on high volume (SOS) for markup to begin.

Bottom Reference — "The Ice" (SC Low):
The low of the Selling Climax.
This becomes the BOTTOM of the accumulation range.
The Spring dips BELOW this level briefly.
Any close significantly below this level invalidates the SC.
```

### 13.2 Phase A — Stopping the Downtrend

**Purpose:** Halt the prior downtrend. The first meaningful demand appears. The range boundaries are defined.

**Duration:** Typically 3–8 sessions (short, concentrated)

**Events in Phase A:**

**A.1 — Preliminary Support (PS)**

*The first noticeable slowdown in the downtrend.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Moderate to wide (down bar, but wider than recent bars)
Volume:  Elevated (first meaningful demand interest)
Close:   Middle to upper half (partial recovery)
Background: Downtrend of 8+ weeks, volume declining on prior bars

Mechanics: First significant buying interest meets the declining supply.
           Price still closes below the session's midpoint in many cases.
           Volume elevated but NOT climactic.

Significance: OBSERVATION only. Do NOT trade this.
              It signals the downtrend MAY be tiring.
              Not all accumulations show a clear PS before the SC.
```

**A.2 — Selling Climax (SC)**

*The bottom event. Maximum fear. Climactic selling absorbed by professional demand.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Widest bar in the prior 4–8 weeks (or longer)
Volume:  Climactic — highest in 4–8 weeks (typically > 5× recent average)
Close:   Middle to upper half of the bar (significant lower wick recovery)
Background: Extended downtrend, prior volume declining on down-bars, PS visible

Mechanics:
→ Every remaining retail seller panics simultaneously
→ SL-M orders cascade through a thinned order book
→ Professional buyer absorbs ALL selling at the low
→ Wide range = market impact of massive order flow sweeping the book
→ Lower wick = intrabar recovery as demand absorbed the low
→ Close in middle = sellers were very strong (wide range) BUT
   buyers were strong enough to recover price significantly from the low

NSE-specific checks:
→ Delivery %: Should be elevated (> 50%) — confirms positional buying
→ FII/DII data: Any net institutional buying that day?
→ Any bulk/block deals? (If so, distort the SC reading)
→ F&O expiry? (Distorted volume — apply caution)

Phase A stops here if SC is clear. If not clear, more downside possible.
```

**A.3 — Automatic Rally (AR)**

*The first significant bounce from the SC.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Moderate to wide (up bars)
Volume:  LOWER than the SC (crucial — confirms SC was genuine)
Close:   Upper half of each bar in the AR sequence
Background: Immediately follows the SC

Mechanics:
→ SC exhausted the supply — sellers just sold everything they had
→ Short sellers who shorted into the SC now cover (buying)
→ With supply gone, even modest demand drives price up rapidly
→ AR defines the TOP of the accumulation range (the Creek)

Duration: Typically 2–8 sessions
Expected move: 8–15% from SC low in most cases

CRITICAL RULE: AR volume must be LOWER than SC volume.
If AR volume > SC volume:
→ More demand than the SC had supply = unusual
→ More likely: the SC was not the genuine bottom
→ Another, more extreme SC may follow
```

**A.4 — Secondary Test (ST)**

*The market revisits the SC area to confirm supply is absent.*

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow (much narrower than SC)
Volume:  LOW — often the lowest in 15–20+ sessions
Close:   Upper half of the ST bar (recovers from the test)
Background: Follows the AR; price declines from AR high toward SC area

Mechanics:
→ Price falls back from the AR high (natural reaction after the bounce)
→ As it approaches the SC area, volume dries up completely
→ Ultra-low volume = almost no sellers present at this level
→ Demand holds price above the SC low — close in upper half
→ Next session: price moves away from the test level

Criteria for a PASSING ST:
✓ Volume is the LOWEST (or near-lowest) of the past 15–20 sessions
✓ Price holds ABOVE the SC low (or breaches only minimally — < 1%)
✓ Close in upper half of the ST bar
✓ Following session moves AWAY from the test area (does not immediately retest)

Criteria for a FAILING ST (SC not genuine):
✗ Volume is elevated (supply HAS returned)
✗ Price closes BELOW the SC low significantly
✗ Following session continues lower
Implication: The SC was Preliminary Support, not the final SC.
             More decline is likely ahead.
```

**Phase A Completion:**
Once the SC, AR, and ST are visible and the ST has passed its criteria, Phase A is complete. The range is defined:
- Range Top = AR High
- Range Bottom = SC Low

### 13.3 Phase B — Building the Cause

**Purpose:** The Composite Operator accumulates their full position. Supply is methodically absorbed.

**Duration:** The LONGEST phase — can be weeks to months. Larger positions = longer Phase B.

**What happens in Phase B:**

```
Price action: Oscillates within the range (between SC low and AR high)
              Each oscillation slightly less extreme than the prior one
              Range narrows over time as both supply and demand moderate

Volume: DECLINING overall
        Occasional absorption spikes (when supply briefly reappears)
        Up-day volume trending higher than down-day volume
        Delivery % steadily rising

VSA events inside Phase B:
→ Multiple Secondary Tests (ST) — each on progressively lower volume
→ Absorption bars (narrow range, high volume at the SC low area)
→ No Supply bars (narrow, low volume, upper close mid-range)
→ Minor Upthrusts within the range (false breaks above the Creek)
   — these are quickly reversed, keeping shorts uncertain

The story of Phase B in plain language:
"The Composite Operator has a shopping list. They want to buy X million
shares without driving the price up against themselves. So they buy
gradually: whenever a seller appears at ₹200 (the SC level), they buy.
Whenever price drifts up to ₹240 (the AR level), they allow it —
even sell a little to prevent price escaping before they're done.
This goes on for weeks or months until they've bought everything on
their list and supply is exhausted."
```

**Phase B diagnostic checklist:**

- [ ] Price is oscillating within the SC Low to AR High range
- [ ] Overall volume is declining from Phase A levels
- [ ] Each test of the SC Low area occurs on LOWER volume than the prior test
- [ ] Delivery % shows a gradual rising trend
- [ ] Price is making HH and HL within the range (internal structure improving)
- [ ] No major news events distorting the volume picture

**The time in Phase B predicts the magnitude of Phase E (Markup):**

```
Phase B duration (weeks): 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20+
Expected markup (approx): 15% | 25–35% | 40–60% | 60–90% | 100%+

These are rough approximations. P&F counting provides more precise targets.
The key point: PATIENCE in Phase B is PROPORTIONAL to reward in Phase E.
```

### 13.4 Phase C — Testing Supply (The Final Exam)

**Purpose:** One final test to confirm that ALL supply has been absorbed. The Spring.

**Duration:** 1–5 sessions (concentrated)

**Events in Phase C:**

**C.1 — The Spring (or Shakeout)**

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow to moderate on the Spring bar itself
Volume:  LOW for Type 1; HIGH but recovering for Type 2
Close:   Upper half — recovered BACK ABOVE the SC Low level
Background: Phase B complete; price approaches the SC Low again

Mechanics:
→ Price dips BELOW the SC Low (the range support / "Ice")
→ This triggers: SL orders from traders with stops below support
→ This attracts: New short sellers expecting breakdown
→ But: supply is GONE (exhausted in Phases A and B)
→ Result: Price immediately snaps back above the SC Low
→ The Spring is the proof that no real sellers remain at or below this level

TYPE 1 SPRING (highest confidence):
→ Volume LOW on the dip (RelVol < 0.7)
→ Price recovers in the SAME session or the NEXT
→ Close WELL above the SC Low
→ Interpretation: Nobody was there to sell below support

TYPE 2 SPRING (moderate confidence):
→ Volume HIGH on the dip (supply DID appear below support)
→ But demand absorbed all of it instantly
→ Close recovers above SC Low despite heavy volume
→ Interpretation: Final supply appeared and was absorbed — now truly exhausted
→ Requires: A positive Test after the Type 2 Spring (crucial)
```

**C.2 — The Positive Test (of the Spring)**

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow (very small range)
Volume:  ULTRA-LOW — often the lowest RelVol in 20+ sessions
Close:   Upper half (holds well above SC Low)
Background: Immediately after the Spring

Mechanics:
→ After the Spring, the market re-tests the Spring area
→ Volume is even LOWER than the Spring itself
→ This tells us: No sellers appeared even on this second probe
→ Supply is confirmed absent — accumulation is complete

This is the HIGHEST confidence signal in the entire accumulation:
→ Ultra-low volume + holds above SC Low + upper close
→ Three simultaneous confirmations of zero supply

A passing Test after a Spring = Phase C complete
```

### 13.5 Phase D — Demand Takes Control

**Purpose:** Demand definitively overpowers supply. Price structure turns bullish.

**Events in Phase D:**

**D.1 — Sign of Strength (SOS) / Jump Across the Creek (JAC)**

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Wide up bar (> 1.5× ATR, often 2–3× ATR)
Volume:  High (2–4× recent average)
Close:   Top third (> 75% of bar's range)
Background: Phase C complete (Spring + Test passed)

Mechanics:
→ Demand now has NO opposition — supply exhausted in Phases A/B/C
→ Any buying immediately drives price higher (no sellers absorbing)
→ Price JUMPS above the Creek (AR High) on wide range, high volume
→ This is the public announcement that accumulation is over

The Creek metaphor:
"Imagine the accumulation range as a river (the Creek) that blocked
northward progress. After exhausting all the supply that was 'in
the river,' demand finally JUMPS ACROSS — the wide up bar on high
volume that closes above the AR High. The Creek is crossed."

SOS criteria checklist:
✓ Volume 2–4× recent average
✓ Range wide (> 1.5× ATR)  
✓ Close in top 75%+ of range
✓ Bar closes ABOVE the AR High (Creek)
✓ Prior Phase C confirmed (Spring + Test)
✓ Delivery % elevated
```

**D.2 — Last Point of Support (LPS)**

```
Four-variable signature:
Spread:  Narrow (small range on the pullback)
Volume:  LOW (< 0.7× average — no supply returning)
Close:   Upper half (holds above old Creek resistance)
Background: After SOS; price pulls back to the Creek level

Mechanics:
→ After jumping the Creek (SOS), price cannot sustain vertical movement
→ A normal pullback occurs to the prior resistance (Creek)
→ The Creek (old resistance) is now tested as SUPPORT
→ Low volume on the pullback = no supply returned to the newly broken level
→ Old resistance has BECOME support (role reversal — Chapter 7)

The LPS is the final confirmation that the breakout is genuine:
"The floor that was once the ceiling has held."

LPS criteria checklist:
✓ Follows SOS within 2–8 sessions
✓ Volume LOW (< 0.7× average, ideally lowest since the Spring)
✓ Price holds ABOVE the Creek (AR High level)
✓ Close in upper half of the LPS bar
✓ Following session moves away from LPS level upward

THE LPS IS THE OPTIMAL TRADE ENTRY IN THE ENTIRE WYCKOFF CYCLE.
```

**D.3 — Back Up to the Edge of the Creek (BUEC)**

```
Mechanics: A second (or third) pullback to the Creek level during Phase D.
           Similar to the LPS but occurs AFTER the initial markup has begun.
           Volume on BUEC: LOW (same as LPS criteria)
           Close: Upper half

Significance: Each successful BUEC confirms the new support level.
              Later BUECs have higher confidence but lower reward than the LPS.
```

## LEVEL 2 — INTERMEDIATE

### 13.6 Phase E — Markup and Trade Management

**Purpose:** Price advances toward the Cause-Effect target. The Composite Operator holds (and eventually plans to distribute at a higher price).

**Markup characteristics:**

```
Price structure: HH/HL sequence (Chapter 7 uptrend)
Volume pattern: Expanding on up-legs, contracting on pullbacks
Duration: Weeks to years (proportional to Phase B duration)

Within Phase E:
→ Re-accumulation ranges (smaller ranges mid-markup — Chapter 12)
   Each re-accumulation has the same structure as primary accumulation
   but shorter in duration, at a higher price level
→ Each re-accumulation adds ADDITIONAL cause to the original
→ Combined cause = original accumulation + all re-accumulations
→ Total target increases with each re-accumulation
```

**Phase E early-warning signals of approaching distribution:**

```
Signal 1: Volume divergence
→ Up-wave volumes declining (each new high on less participation)
→ Down-wave volumes rising (each pullback has more selling)
→ Combined: demand fading + supply growing

Signal 2: Spread divergence
→ Up-bars becoming narrower for same or greater volume
→ (High effort producing less result on up-bars)

Signal 3: Close location deterioration
→ Up-bars increasingly closing in middle or lower portion of range
→ (Sellers active during up-sessions, limiting close location)

Signal 4: Structural warning (Market Structure — Chapter 7)
→ CHoCH appears (price breaks a prior Higher Low)
→ This is the structural warning to reduce/exit

Signal 5: BC characteristics
→ Extreme volume + wide up range + close in lower half
→ This marks the end of Phase E and the beginning of Phase A of Distribution
```

### 13.7 Non-Classic Accumulation Variations

Real NSE markets rarely produce textbook-perfect schematics. These are the common variations and how to read them.

**Variation 1: Accumulation Without a Spring (Phase C Skipped)**

```
When: Supply was so thoroughly absorbed in Phase B that no final
      test below support is needed.

Evidence this is occurring:
→ Multiple STs in Phase B, each on progressively lower volume
→ Final STs show near-zero volume (0.2–0.3×)
→ No Supply bars dominating Phase B's latter portion
→ SOS appears directly from within the range without a dip below

Trade implication:
→ Higher risk than classic (no Spring flush-out of weak holders)
→ Require SOS volume to be particularly high (3.5×+ minimum)
→ LPS must be on extremely low volume to compensate
→ Position size: Reduce by 25% vs classic setup

Confidence: Moderate to High (7–8/9 on Nine Tests, depending on STS)
```

**Variation 2: Multiple Springs (Phase C Extends)**

```
When: Supply repeatedly reappears just below the SC Low, requiring
      multiple Spring tests.

Evidence:
→ Spring 1 occurs, but volume is moderate (Type 2) — supply still present
→ Price rallies back into range but fails to produce strong SOS
→ Falls again below SC Low — SPRING 2 (should show lower volume than Spring 1)
→ If Spring 2 volume is lower and recovery stronger → accumulation continuing
→ If Spring 2 volume is HIGHER → genuine breakdown (SC was not final bottom)

Trade implication:
→ Wait for the LAST Spring to show the lowest volume
→ The series: Spring 1 (moderate vol) → Spring 2 (low vol) → Test (ultra-low)
→ Do NOT enter until the final test confirms ultra-low volume

Confidence: Moderate until final low-volume test passes
```

**Variation 3: Accumulation in a Rising Market (Stealth Accumulation)**

```
When: The CO accumulates while the market is already in mild markup.
      The stock moves sideways while the market rises.

Evidence:
→ Stock underperforms Nifty during its accumulation (relative weakness while
   accumulating, then relative strength at SOS)
→ No dramatic SC (because the prior downtrend was short or mild)
→ Range is tighter than classic accumulation
→ Volume pattern shows absorption within the range

Trade implication:
→ Less dramatic SC means less cause built in Phase A
→ Rely more heavily on Phase B duration for cause estimate
→ Nine Tests score may be lower (Test 1 and 2 may not pass clearly)
→ Require strong Nine Tests 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 to compensate

Confidence: Moderate (requires more Phase B duration to compensate for mild SC)
```

**Variation 4: Failed Accumulation (Phase B Breakdown)**

```
When: The accumulation FAILS — a new low is made with HIGH volume and
      price does NOT recover.

Signs of failure:
→ A test of the SC Low produces HIGH volume (supply returning)
→ Price closes BELOW the SC Low and remains there for 2+ sessions
→ Volume on the break below the SC Low is equal to or greater than the SC itself
→ Following sessions make new lows

Interpretation:
→ The SC was actually Preliminary Support (PS) — more SC ahead
→ The accumulation thesis is INVALIDATED
→ Exit any long positions immediately (stop triggered)
→ The next genuine SC will occur at a lower level

NSE practical rule: If price closes BELOW the SC Low on any session
                    with volume GREATER than 2× the average,
                    the accumulation thesis is invalidated.
                    Exit without exception.
```

### 13.8 Accumulation Trade Planning — The Three Entry Zones

![Accumulation Trade Planning — Entry Zones, Stops, and Targets](/images/pi-accumulation-trade-zones.jpg)

There are three valid entry points within the Wyckoff accumulation:

**Entry Zone 1 (E1) — Spring Entry**

```
Timing: During Phase C — after the Spring bar closes back above the SC Low

Trigger:
→ Spring bar's close: Back above the SC Low support level
→ Volume on the Spring: LOW (RelVol < 0.7 for Type 1)
→ The NEXT session: Closes higher than the Spring's close

Entry price: On the close of the bar AFTER the Spring (confirmation bar)
             Or: At the open of the session 2 sessions after the Spring
             (allowing the Test to form first)

Stop-loss (SL): Below the SPRING LOW
                (Not below the SC Low — below the actual low of the Spring bar)
                This is a tighter stop than the SC Low, improving risk-reward

Target 1 (T1): The AR High (Creek level) — approximately 8–15% above entry
Target 2 (T2): The Cause-Effect target (1× range above breakout)

Position size: 50% of intended full position
              (Spring is earlier — more uncertainty remains)

Risk-Reward: Typically 1:3 to 1:6 (tight stop, large move ahead)

Rationale for partial position:
→ Spring can fail (Type 2 Spring, failed test, re-test needed)
→ Wait to see if SOS appears before committing fully
→ Add to the position at the LPS (E2) if the Spring is confirmed
```

**Entry Zone 2 (E2) — LPS Entry (Optimal)**

```
Timing: During Phase D — after SOS, on the pullback to the Creek

Trigger:
→ SOS has occurred (all criteria met — Chapter 11)
→ Pullback to the Creek level begins
→ Volume dries up on the pullback (RelVol < 0.7)
→ Pullback bar closes in upper half above the Creek level
→ Following session begins to move away from the Creek upward

Entry price: At the close of the LPS bar, or at the open of the
             following session (after confirming the LPS holds)

Stop-loss (SL): Below the LPS Low (tightest stop)
                OR: Below the SOS bar's midpoint (moderate stop)
                OR: Below the Creek level (widest stop, lowest risk of being stopped)

Target 1 (T1): 1× Range height above the SOS breakout level
Target 2 (T2): 2× Range height (for Phase B durations > 8 weeks)
Target 3 (T3): Cause-Effect P&F count target (most precise)

Position size: 75–100% of intended full position
              (LPS is the highest-confidence setup)

Risk-Reward: Typically 1:4 to 1:8

Rationale for full position:
→ SOS is confirmed (Phase D evidence)
→ LPS holding = final confirmation of role reversal
→ Risk is now defined by a specific level (LPS Low)
→ The accumulation thesis has maximum evidence behind it
```

**Entry Zone 3 (E3) — BUEC Entry (Add-on)**

```
Timing: During Phase E early markup — after LPS, second pullback to Creek

Trigger:
→ Markup is underway (price above the Creek and rising)
→ A pullback returns to the Creek level again
→ Volume dries up (ultra-low) on the BUEC pullback
→ Close in upper half, holds above Creek

Entry price: At the close of the BUEC bar or next session open

Stop-loss (SL): Below the BUEC Low (very tight)

Target: Next markup target (based on any re-accumulation causes)

Position size: 25–50% ADDITION to existing LPS position
              (Not a new full position — an add-on)

Risk-Reward: 1:3 to 1:5

Rationale:
→ Markup is confirmed (price has moved in the expected direction)
→ BUEC confirms the Creek remains support
→ Adding here increases exposure to a confirmed trade
→ Risk is minimal (markup is established)
```

**The professional position building sequence:**

```
E1 (Spring): 50% position — early, tighter stop, higher RR but more uncertainty
E2 (LPS): Add to 75–100% — optimal setup, confirmed thesis
E3 (BUEC): Optional add — final confirmation, lowest risk

Average cost calculation example:
Spring at ₹202 (50% position): average cost contribution = ₹101
LPS at ₹248 (add to 100%): average cost contribution = ₹124
Total average cost: ₹225 (on full position)

Spring Low = ₹196 (hard stop on Spring portion)
LPS Low = ₹243 (trailing stop after LPS entry)

This staged approach: Lower average entry cost vs all-in at LPS
                     Smaller initial risk vs all-in at Spring
```

### 13.9 Trade Management Through Markup

**Stop management:**

```
Stage 1 (Spring entry): Hard stop = Spring Low
Stage 2 (LPS entry): Raise stop to just below the LPS Low
Stage 3 (BUEC entry): Raise stop to just below the BUEC Low
Stage 4 (First re-accumulation forms): Raise stop to below re-accum range low
Stage 5 (Each subsequent LPS in markup): Raise stop to each new LPS low

Principle: The stop moves UP with each new confirmed LPS in the markup.
           You are NEVER taking more risk than required for the current
           stage of the markup.
```

**Profit-taking rules:**

```
T1 (First target): Sell 25–33% of position at 1× range extension
                   Lock in profit; reduce size as risk increases near resistance

T2 (Second target): Sell another 25–33% at 2× range extension (if Phase B > 8 wks)

T3 (Distribution signals): Exit remaining position when ANY of these appear:
→ Volume divergence (decreasing volume on new highs — 3+ consecutive rally waves)
→ BC characteristics (extreme volume + upper wick + low close)
→ CHoCH on daily chart (breaks below a prior Higher Low)
→ Nine Selling Tests scoring 6+ out of 9 (distribution confirmed)
```

**The trailing stop using market structure:**

```
In markup, each Higher Low (HL) is a potential stop level.
Professional rule: Place trailing stop 1–2% below the most recent HL.
When price makes a new HL that holds for 2+ sessions: Raise stop to below new HL.

This creates a mechanical, non-discretionary trailing stop
that aligns precisely with the Wyckoff markup structure.
```

## LEVEL 3 — ADVANCED / PROFESSIONAL

### 13.10 Sub-Phase Analysis — Reading the Schematic in Real Time

In practice, you are reading the schematic WITHOUT knowing where you are. You see the current bar but not the future. The professional skill is REAL-TIME phase identification.

**The real-time decision tree:**

```
STEP 1: Is there a prior downtrend of ≥ 8 weeks?
→ YES: Potential Phase A beginning
→ NO: Possible re-accumulation (smaller cause) or not an accumulation

STEP 2: Has a climactic event occurred (wide spread, extreme volume, wick recovery)?
→ YES: SC identified → Phase A complete → Enter Phase B watch
→ NO: Still in downtrend OR PS only → Wait for SC

STEP 3: Has the AR defined a clear range top? Has an ST occurred on low volume?
→ BOTH YES: Phase A complete, Phase B confirmed
→ ST on high volume: SC may not be genuine → Wait for lower SC

STEP 4: Is price oscillating between SC Low and AR High with declining volume?
→ YES: Phase B ongoing → Count duration, watch delivery %
→ Breakout of range on HIGH volume to DOWNSIDE: Failed accumulation → No trade

STEP 5: Has a Spring occurred? (Dip below SC Low + recovery)
→ Type 1 Spring (low volume + recovery): Phase C → Wait for Test
→ Type 2 Spring (high volume + recovery): Phase C → Test REQUIRED before entry
→ No Spring + price rising from range midpoint: Possible no-Spring accumulation

STEP 6: Has a Test confirmed? (Ultra-low volume probe + upper close)
→ YES: Phase C complete → Watch for SOS
→ NO: Phase C incomplete → Do NOT enter yet

STEP 7: Has the SOS broken above the Creek on high volume?
→ YES: Phase D begins → Wait for LPS
→ Volume on SOS is low (< 1.5×): Questionable SOS → Wait for second attempt

STEP 8: Has the LPS formed? (Low volume pullback to Creek level + holds)
→ YES: OPTIMAL ENTRY (E2) → Execute trade plan
→ Pullback is on HIGH volume: LPS failed → SOS may not be genuine

STEP 9: Is markup underway? (HH/HL above the Creek)
→ YES: Phase E → Manage the trade with trailing stops
→ BUEC forming: E3 add-on opportunity
```

### 13.11 Accumulation Schematic Applied to NSE Indices

The accumulation schematic is most powerful when applied to the NIFTY 50 INDEX itself — because the index represents the aggregate of all large-cap stocks. When Nifty forms a genuine accumulation, virtually every Nifty constituent benefits from the subsequent markup.

**NSE index accumulation identification:**

```
Phase A signals on Nifty:
→ SC: High Nifty futures volume on a wide down-day; FII net buy that day
→ AR: Nifty bounces 3–5% from SC low with declining futures volume
→ ST: Nifty retests SC area on LOW futures volume, holds above SC low

Phase B signals on Nifty:
→ FII net buying trend: Sustained net FII buying over weeks
→ Put/Call ratio: Rising (options market pricing higher probability of continued down)
   This creates the "wall of worry" — retail is pessimistic while FII is accumulating
→ Advance/Decline line: Improving even while Nifty oscillates (breadth improving)

Phase C signals on Nifty:
→ Spring: Nifty briefly makes a new low then recovers sharply on FII buying
→ This is often reported as "FIIs bought the dip" in financial media
→ Test: Ultra-low Nifty futures volume session holding above Spring low

Phase D / E:
→ SOS: Nifty futures volume expands as price breaks above accumulation range
→ FII data confirms net buying escalating
→ Sector rotation: Leaders emerge (Banks, Autos, IT leading the markup)
```

## QUIZ ANSWERS

### Conceptual Answers

**A1.** Two reference levels: (1) The Creek = the AR High = Top of the accumulation range. Named for the river metaphor — the resistance that must be "jumped" by the SOS. Used as: the target for the Spring entry (T1), the support level for the LPS and BUEC, and the visual confirmation level for the SOS. (2) The Ice = the SC Low = Bottom of the accumulation range. Named because it is fragile and can be "broken" (the Spring temporarily breaks through the Ice before recovering). Used as: the boundary that the Spring must dip below (briefly) and return above, the hard stop for Spring entries, and the invalidation level (if price closes below and stays there, accumulation fails).

**A2.** AR volume must be lower than SC volume because: The AR is driven by the ABSENCE of supply (sellers exhausted themselves in the SC), not by new massive demand. With no sellers, even modest buying drives price easily. If AR volume EXCEEDS SC volume: (1) Demand surged MORE than the supply at the SC — rare and meaningful, suggesting the SC attracted even more demand than selling. (2) More likely: The SC was only Preliminary Support — supply was NOT exhausted. More selling will come when price recovers to the AR high. A higher-volume AR after a suspected SC = watch for a SECOND, more dramatic SC at or below the first SC's low.

**A3.** Type 1 Spring: Dip below SC Low on LOW volume (RelVol < 0.7). The supply below support was virtually absent — nobody wanted to sell. Instant recovery same session. Highest confidence — directly proves no supply below support. Type 2 Spring: Dip below SC Low on HIGH volume. Supply DID appear below support, but was instantly absorbed by demand. Recovery occurs but involves a "fight." Requires confirmation. Additional confirmation = a Positive Test after the Type 2 Spring: a subsequent probe of the Spring level on ULTRA-LOW volume (even lower than the Spring's volume), with close in the upper half. This confirms the supply that appeared in the Type 2 Spring has now been fully absorbed.

**A4.** BUEC (Back Up to the Edge of the Creek): A second or subsequent pullback to the Creek level AFTER the LPS, during early Phase E markup. Differs from LPS: (1) Occurs AFTER markup has already begun (price has moved above the Creek, made new HHs, then pulled back). (2) The Creek has been "tested" multiple times — increasing confidence that it is support. (3) Risk is even lower than the LPS (markup has already begun, confirmation higher). Position management significance: The BUEC is the optimal point to ADD to the position (E3 entry zone). It confirms the Creek continues to hold and provides a tight, well-defined stop level.

**A5.** Failed accumulation: A price event where the SC Low is penetrated on HIGH volume and price DOES NOT RECOVER. Specific bar event triggering invalidation: Price closes BELOW the SC Low on volume GREATER than 2× the 20-day average (and remains below on the following session). Correct response: Exit any long positions IMMEDIATELY. The SC was Preliminary Support, not the final SC. More decline is ahead — the actual SC will occur at a lower price. Do not "average down" or "wait for recovery." The Wyckoff rule is clear: close below SC Low on high volume = exit without exception.

**A6.** LPS is optimal because four conditions are simultaneously true: (1) The SOS has CONFIRMED the accumulation thesis — Phase D is underway, the range is broken to the upside. The probability of the accumulation being genuine is at its highest. (2) Volume on the LPS is LOW — no supply has returned to the new support level (old Creek). Directly proves role reversal is complete. (3) Risk is DEFINED — stop is placed below the LPS Low (a specific, measurable level). No ambiguity about where you're wrong. (4) Price is NEAR the new support (Creek level) — the ratio of remaining upside (full markup ahead) to downside (LPS Low to Creek) is excellent. All four being true simultaneously makes the LPS the ideal risk-reward moment in the cycle.

**A7.** Phase B primary purpose: The Composite Operator accumulates their full desired position by absorbing every unit of supply that appears at low prices in the range, over a sustained period of time. Two non-price metrics tracking Phase B progress most reliably on NSE: (1) Delivery % — should rise from Phase A levels (~25–35%) to Phase B end (~55–65%), confirming committed positional buyers are accumulating and holding. (2) FII/DII net flow data — sustained institutional net buying over weeks while price is range-bound is the closest external confirmation of CO-style accumulation activity. Both metrics should trend in the bullish direction throughout Phase B.

**A8.** Accumulation without a Spring: Supply was so thoroughly absorbed during Phase B that no final flush-out below support was needed. Phase C is skipped. Compensating evidence required: (1) SOS must be on particularly high volume (≥ 3.5× average) — because without the Spring flush, more weak holders remain; the SOS needs extra force to convince them. (2) Multiple STs in Phase B, each on progressively lower volume — the final ST should show near-zero volume, compensating for the absent Spring. (3) LPS must be on ULTRA-LOW volume — confirming the SOS level is genuine support without the Spring's validation. (4) Nine Tests score should be 7+/9 despite the missing Spring criterion.

**A9.** Stop progression: (1) Spring entry hard stop: Below the SPRING LOW (the actual intrabar low of the Spring bar — NOT the SC Low). (2) After SOS: Raise stop to just below the SOS bar's midpoint (or to just below the Creek level — the old resistance). (3) After LPS: Raise stop to just below the LPS LOW (tighter). (4) After each re-accumulation's LPS: Raise stop to below that LPS low. (5) Phase E trailing stop: 1–2% below the most recent confirmed Higher Low in the markup. This creates a mechanical, structure-based trailing stop that always moves with the market structure and never requires discretion.

**A10.** Five early-warning signals that markup is ending: (1) Volume divergence: Decreasing volume on successive up-waves (demand fading). (2) Spread divergence: Up-bars narrowing for same or greater volume (effort producing less result). (3) Close location deterioration: Up-bars closing in middle/lower portion (sellers increasingly active during sessions). (4) Structural CHoCH: Price breaks a prior Higher Low for the first time (Market Structure signal — Chapter 7). (5) BC characteristics: Extreme volume + wide up range + close in lower half (climactic distribution). Highest structural importance: #4 — the CHoCH. It is the FIRST structural confirmation that the uptrend has ended, not just slowed. All other signals are WARNINGS that precede the CHoCH; the CHoCH is the CONFIRMATION.

## KEY TAKEAWAYS — CHAPTER 13

> **1. The accumulation schematic has five phases (A–E) each with a distinct purpose: A=Stopping the downtrend; B=Building the cause; C=Testing supply (Spring); D=Demand takes control (SOS+LPS); E=Markup.**

> **2. Two reference lines anchor everything: The Creek (AR High = range top) and The Ice (SC Low = range bottom). The Spring dips below the Ice; the SOS jumps above the Creek; the LPS holds the Creek as new support.**

> **3. There are three valid entry zones: E1 (Spring — 50% position, highest RR, highest uncertainty), E2 (LPS — full position, optimal RR, confirmed setup), E3 (BUEC — add-on, lowest risk, lowest incremental reward).**

> **4. The LPS is the OPTIMAL entry: SOS confirmed + low-volume pullback to old resistance + holds above Creek. Four simultaneous conditions = highest probability, most defined risk.**

> **5. Phase B is the most important phase for the MAGNITUDE of the eventual move. Longer Phase B = larger cause = larger effect. Delivery % and institutional flow data track Phase B's progress.**

> **6. Non-classic variations (no Spring, multiple Springs, stealth accumulation) exist. Adjust confidence and position sizing accordingly. A missing Spring requires the SOS and LPS to be on particularly high/low volume respectively.**

> **7. Stop management: Spring Hard Stop → SOS-level stop → LPS-level stop → each new Higher Low in markup. Never give back more than one swing's worth of profit. Exit at BC characteristics or CHoCH.**
