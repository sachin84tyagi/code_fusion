## 📖 Part 1 — The 5 Survival Words

---

### 🟢 Word 1 — JA (Yes)

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **German** | ja |
| **Meaning** | Yes / Haan |
| **Pronunciation** | **yaa** |
| **Important** | J = Y sound in German (Mission 001-A mein seekha tha!) |

**Usage examples:**

| German | Meaning |
|:-------|:--------|
| Ja, danke. | Yes, thank you. |
| Ja, bitte. | Yes, please. |
| Ja, natürlich. | Yes, of course. |

> 🧠 **Memory trick:** "Ja" sounds exactly like the Hindi **"yaa"** (जा). Same word, same vibe — just means "yes" instead of "go"! 😄

---

### 🔴 Word 2 — NEIN (No)

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **German** | nein |
| **Meaning** | No / Nahi |
| **Pronunciation** | **nine** (like the number 9!) |
| **Important** | ei = eye sound (Mission 002!) |

**Usage examples:**

| German | Meaning |
|:-------|:--------|
| Nein, danke. | No, thank you. |
| Nein, nein, nein! | No, no, no! |
| Nein, das ist falsch. | No, that is wrong. |

> 🧠 **Memory trick:** **NEIN** sounds exactly like the English number **NINE**. So when someone asks you something in German and you want to say no — just say **"nine"**! 

> ⚠️ **Don't confuse:** **nein** (no) vs **neun** (nine, the number). They sound very similar but spelled differently!
> - **nein** = no → "nine"
> - **neun** = 9 → "noyn"

---

### 🙏 Word 3 — BITTE (Please / You're Welcome)

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **German** | bitte |
| **Meaning** | Please / You're welcome / Here you go |
| **Pronunciation** | **BIT-teh** |
| **Superpower** | One word — THREE different uses! |

**Use 1: Please (request karte waqt)**

| German | Meaning |
|:-------|:--------|
| Einen Kaffee, bitte. | A coffee, please. |
| Die Rechnung, bitte. | The bill, please. |
| Sprechen Sie langsam, bitte. | Please speak slowly. |

**Use 2: You're welcome (Danke ke jawab mein)**

| German | Meaning |
|:-------|:--------|
| — Danke! — Bitte! | — Thank you! — You're welcome! |

**Use 3: Here you go / Please (kuch dete waqt)**

| Situation | What you say |
|:----------|:------------|
| Waiter aapko khaana de raha hai | "Bitte!" (here you go!) |
| Koi door khol ke deta hai | "Bitte!" (please / here you go!) |

> 🧠 **Memory trick:** Think of **"bitter"** tea — but Germans are very polite, so they drop the 'r' and say **"bitte"** instead! ☕

> 💡 **Cultural note:** "Bitte" is one of the most versatile words in German. Jitna zyada use karo, utna polite lagta hai. Germans appreciate politeness a lot.

---

### 🙏 Word 4 — DANKE (Thank You)

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **German** | danke |
| **Meaning** | Thank you / Shukriya |
| **Pronunciation** | **DANK-eh** |
| **Upgrade** | Danke schön = Thank you very much |

**Levels of thanks:**

| German | Meaning | When to use |
|:-------|:--------|:------------|
| **Danke.** | Thank you. | Normal situations |
| **Danke schön.** | Thank you very much. | When more grateful |
| **Danke sehr.** | Thank you very much. | Same as above |
| **Vielen Dank.** | Many thanks. | Formal / very grateful |

> 🧠 **Memory trick:** **DANKE** sounds like the English word **"donkey"** — but without the "ey" at the end. Imagine a donkey bowing politely and saying thank you. 🫏

> ⚙️ **Pronunciation reminder:** The final **"e"** in German is never silent (unlike English). **Danke** = **DANK-eh**, not "dank".

---

### 😬 Word 5 — ENTSCHULDIGUNG (Excuse Me / Sorry)

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **German** | Entschuldigung |
| **Meaning** | Excuse me / Sorry / Pardon |
| **Pronunciation** | **ent-SHOOL-di-goong** |
| **Reality check** | It's a long word. But it's worth it. |

**Breaking it down syllable by syllable:**

```
ENT  —  SCHUL  —  DI  —  GUNG
 ent  —   shool  —  di  —  goong
```

Say each part slowly first, then speed up:
- **ent** → like "ent" in "entertainment"
- **schul** → "shool" (sch = sh, remember?)
- **di** → "dee"
- **gung** → "goong"

Full word: **ent-SHOOL-dee-goong**

**When to use it:**

| Situation | German |
|:----------|:-------|
| Kisi se raasta poochna | "Entschuldigung, wo ist...?" |
| Bheed mein se nikalna | "Entschuldigung!" |
| Galti ho gayi | "Entschuldigung!" |
| Kisi ka dhyan kheenchna | "Entschuldigung, bitte." |

> 🧠 **Break it down trick:** **"Ent-SCHOOL-dee-goong"** — imagine you're late to SCHOOL, say "Ent-SCHOOL" and run! 🏫

> 💡 **Shortcut:** In casual situations, Germans often just say **"Entschuldige"** (ent-SHOOL-dee-geh) — slightly shorter. Both work.

## 📖 Part 2 — Combining the 5 Words

The real power comes when you COMBINE these words. Watch how far just 5 words take you:

### Scenario A — Someone offers you coffee ☕

> **Alien:** "Kaffee?" *(Coffee?)*
> **You:** "Ja, bitte!" *(Yes, please!)*
> **Alien:** hands you coffee
> **You:** "Danke schön!" *(Thank you very much!)*
> **Alien:** "Bitte!" *(You're welcome!)*

You just had a complete polite exchange. With 3 words total.

---

### Scenario B — Someone offers something you don't want

> **Alien:** "Kaffee?" *(Coffee?)*
> **You:** "Nein, danke." *(No, thank you.)*

Polite rejection. Done. One sentence.

---

### Scenario C — You need to ask something

> **You:** "Entschuldigung..." *(Excuse me...)*
> **Alien:** turns to look at you
> **You:** "Wo ist...?" *(Where is...?)* ← Mission 009 mein seekhenge
> **Alien:** gives directions
> **You:** "Danke!" *(Thank you!)*

You successfully got help from a stranger. With 3 known words.

---

### Scenario D — You made a mistake

> **You:** accidentally bumps into someone
> **You:** "Entschuldigung!" *(Sorry!)*
> **Alien:** "Bitte." *(No worries / It's okay)*

Crisis averted. One word.

## 📖 Part 3 — 5 More Bonus Words (High Value, Low Effort)

Ye 5 extra words hain jo almost equally important hain. Memorize them now while we're here:

| German | Meaning | Pronunciation | Memory Trick |
|:-------|:--------|:--------------|:-------------|
| **Hallo** | Hello | **HAL-loh** | Exactly like English "Hello"! |
| **Tschüss** | Bye (informal) | **CHÜSS** | Like "Choose" but with ü sound |
| **Auf Wiedersehen** | Goodbye (formal) | **owf VEE-der-zeh-en** | "Auf" = on, "Wieder" = again, "sehen" = see |
| **Gut** | Good / Fine | **goot** | Sounds like "good" but shorter |
| **Okay / OK** | OK | **oh-KAY** | Same as English! Germans use it too |

> 💡 **Cultural note:** Germans use **"Tschüss"** all the time in casual settings — friends, shops, cafes. It's their equivalent of "bye" or "ciao". **"Auf Wiedersehen"** is for formal situations.
