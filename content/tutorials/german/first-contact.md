## 📖 Part 1 — What Did the Robot Just Say?

Let's decode those two lines, one by one.

---

### 🔹 Expression 1 — Guten Morgen

**German:** Guten Morgen
**Meaning:** Good Morning
**Pronunciation:** **GOO-ten MOR-gen**

How to remember it:
- "Guten" sounds a little like "good-en" — it literally means "good"
- "Morgen" sounds like "Morgan" — it means "morning"
- So: **Good + Morning = Guten Morgen** ✅

**When to use it:** Subah uthke kisi ko milne par — exactly like "Good Morning" in English.

> 🎬 **Scene tip:** Imagine the robot's name is Morgan. Every morning Morgan says: *"GOO-ten, Morgan!"* — that's how you remember it.

---

### 🔹 Expression 2 — Willkommen an Bord

**German:** Willkommen an Bord
**Meaning:** Welcome on Board
**Pronunciation:** **vil-KOM-en an BORD**

Breaking it down:
- "Willkommen" = Welcome (sounds very close to English "welcome")
- "an Bord" = on board (same meaning as English "on board")

**When to use it:** Jab koi nayi jagah aaye — like welcoming someone to your spaceship, office, or home.

> 💡 **Memory trick:** "Will come on board" → "Willkommen an Bord" — almost English!

---

### 🔹 Expression 3 — Wie heißt du?

**German:** Wie heißt du?
**Meaning:** What is your name? / Tumhara naam kya hai?
**Pronunciation:** **vee HYSST doo?**

Breaking it down:
- "Wie" = How/What (pronounced like "vee" — the letter V sound)
- "heißt" = is called / named (pronounced "hysst" — the **ß** is like a double-s)
- "du" = you (pronounced "doo")
- Literally: *"How are you called?"*

**When to use it:** Kisi naye insaan se naam poochna ho to.

> 🎬 **Scene tip:** The robot sounds like it's asking: *"Vee HYSST doo?"* — imagine a robot going "HISSSST" like a snake — that's the "heißt" sound. 🐍

## ✍️ Part 2 — Now YOU Answer the Robot

The robot asked: **"Wie heißt du?"** (What is your name?)

Your answer template:

| German | Meaning | Pronunciation |
|:-------|:--------|:--------------|
| **Ich heiße \_\_\_.** | My name is \_\_\_. | **ikh HY-sse \_\_\_.** |

Fill in your own name!

**Example:**
- **Ich heiße Sachin.** → My name is Sachin.
- **Ich heiße Priya.** → My name is Priya.
- **Ich heiße Arjun.** → My name is Arjun.

Breaking it down:
- **Ich** = I (pronounced "ikh" — soft, not like the English "itch")
- **heiße** = am called / my name is
- Then just say your name!

> ⚠️ **Pronunciation note:** "Ich" is tricky. It's NOT "ick" or "ich" like in English. It's a very soft sound made at the back of the throat — a gentle whisper of air. Think: you're fogging up a mirror — that soft "hh" is close. Practice it a few times.

## 🧩 Part 3 — Build the Pattern

Notice something? Look at these two together:

| German | Meaning |
|:-------|:--------|
| **Wie heißt du?** | What is YOUR name? |
| **Ich heiße Sachin.** | MY name is Sachin. |

The key word **"heiß–"** appears in both sentences!

- **heißt** → used when talking ABOUT someone (du = you)
- **heiße** → used when talking AS yourself (ich = I)

Don't memorize this as a rule yet — just notice the pattern. It will make more sense as you see more examples.

### 🔤 Key Pronunciation Points (Watch These!)

- **Ich** → NOT "ick" → soft throaty "ikh" sound
- **Wie** → NOT "wie" like English → say "VEE"
- **heißt / heiße** → the "ß" = double-s → sounds like "hysst / hy-sse"
- **du** → sounds like "doo" (not "du" like in Hindi "do")

### ⚠️ Common Beginner Mistakes

| Mistake | Why Wrong | Correct |
|:--------|:----------|:--------|
| Saying "Ich" like "itch" | English habit | Soft "ikh" — not sharp |
| Saying "Wie" like "why" | English habit | Say "Vee" |
| Forgetting the "e" at end of heiße | It matters! | **hei-sse** not "heisst" when saying YOUR name |
